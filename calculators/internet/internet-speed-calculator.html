Internet Speed Calculator – Mbps, MB/s and Download Time

Calculate what your internet speed actually means in practice. Convert between Mbps (megabits per second) and MB/s (megabytes per second), estimate download times for files and games, and find out if your connection is fast enough for streaming, gaming, video calls, and remote work.

Understanding internet speed helps you choose the right plan, diagnose slow connections, and set realistic expectations for large file downloads. Most internet providers advertise speeds in Mbps, while file sizes are shown in MB or GB — requiring a conversion to understand real-world performance.

Formula

Download Time = File Size (MB) × 8 / Speed (Mbps) | MB/s = Mbps / 8 | GB download time = GB × 1024 × 8 / Mbps / 60 minutes

Understanding Internet Speed Units

Internet speed is measured in megabits per second (Mbps), but file sizes are measured in megabytes (MB). Since 1 byte = 8 bits, your download speed in MB/s is always Mbps divided by 8. A 100 Mbps connection downloads at 12.5 MB/s — a common source of confusion when actual download speeds appear much lower than advertised.

Mbps vs MB/s — The Key Difference

Your internet provider advertises 100 Mbps. Your download manager shows 12 MB/s. Both are correct — 100 Mbps ÷ 8 = 12.5 MB/s. File managers and download software display speeds in MB/s because file sizes are in MB. Always divide your Mbps by 8 to get the MB/s download speed you will actually see.

Actual vs Advertised Speed

ISPs advertise "up to" speeds which are theoretical maximums. Real-world speeds depend on network congestion (peak hours 7-11pm are slowest), WiFi signal strength (WiFi is always slower than wired), router age and capability, number of connected devices, and distance from the ISP node. A 100 Mbps plan typically delivers 70-90 Mbps during off-peak hours.

Upload Speed Matters Too

Most home internet plans are asymmetric — download speed is much faster than upload. Upload speed matters for video calls, live streaming, cloud backups, and remote desktop. Zoom video calls need 3 Mbps upload. Twitch streaming at 1080p60 needs 6 Mbps upload. Cloud backup of 1TB at 10 Mbps upload takes 222 hours — 9 days!

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good internet speed for a household? +
For a family of 4 with multiple simultaneous users streaming, gaming, and working: 200-500 Mbps is comfortable. For a single user or couple: 100 Mbps is plenty. For basic browsing and email only: 25 Mbps is sufficient. Gigabit (1000 Mbps) is future-proof.
Why is my download speed lower than my plan speed? +
ISPs advertise maximum speeds. Real speeds are lower due to: WiFi vs wired connection (WiFi can be 20-50% slower), network congestion during peak hours, router limitations (old routers cap at 100 Mbps), multiple devices sharing bandwidth, and distance from the ISP infrastructure.
What is the difference between Mbps and MB/s? +
Mbps = megabits per second (network speed). MB/s = megabytes per second (file download speed). Since 1 byte = 8 bits, MB/s = Mbps / 8. A 100 Mbps connection downloads files at 12.5 MB/s. This is why your download manager shows lower numbers than your plan speed.
How long does it take to download a 50 GB game? +
At 100 Mbps: 50 GB × 1024 × 8 / 100 = 4,096 seconds = 68 minutes. At 500 Mbps: about 14 minutes. At 1 Gbps: about 7 minutes. Actual times may be longer due to server speed limits and network congestion.
What internet speed do I need for 4K streaming? +
Netflix recommends 25 Mbps for 4K Ultra HD streaming per stream. YouTube 4K: 20 Mbps. Disney+ 4K: 25 Mbps. For a household streaming two 4K streams simultaneously plus gaming and browsing, 100-200 Mbps is recommended.
How do I test my actual internet speed? +
Use Speedtest.net (Ookla), Fast.com (Netflix), or Google speed test (search "speed test"). Run tests at different times — morning, afternoon, evening. Evening (7-11pm) is typically 20-40% slower due to peak usage. Connect via ethernet for most accurate results.
