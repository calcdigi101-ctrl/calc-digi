Email Size Calculator – Attachments, Limits and Delivery

Calculate the total size of your email including all attachments and check whether it meets the size limits for Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo Mail, and other email providers. Avoid bounced emails and find alternatives for large file sharing.

Email attachments are encoded in Base64 format which increases file size by approximately 33%. A 10 MB file attachment becomes roughly 13.3 MB in the email. Most email providers have limits of 10-50 MB per message — knowing these limits prevents frustrating delivery failures.

Formula

Email Size = Base Message + Sum of Attachments × 1.33 (Base64 overhead) | Gmail limit: 25 MB | Outlook: 20-150 MB

Base64 Encoding Overhead

Email attachments must be encoded as text (Base64) to travel through email systems. Base64 converts every 3 bytes of binary data into 4 ASCII characters — a 33% size increase. A 15 MB PDF becomes 20 MB in the email, and a 20 MB video becomes 26.7 MB — potentially exceeding limits even though the original file was under the limit.

Alternatives to Large Email Attachments

For files exceeding email limits: Google Drive or OneDrive (share a link), Dropbox (free 2 GB transfers), WeTransfer (free up to 2 GB per transfer), WhatsApp/Telegram (100 MB per file), FTP or SFTP for business file transfers, or company file sharing platforms (SharePoint, Box, Slack). Cloud sharing is faster, more reliable, and allows version control.

Compressing Attachments

ZIP compression can reduce file sizes significantly: Word/Excel files: 50-70% smaller. Already-compressed files (JPG, MP4, PDF): little reduction. Multiple small files zipped together: saves Base64 overhead (one 33% overhead instead of one per file). Tools: 7-Zip (free, best compression), WinRAR, macOS built-in compression.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the maximum email attachment size for Gmail? +
Gmail allows sending emails up to 25 MB total (including all attachments after Base64 encoding). For larger files, Gmail automatically offers to upload to Google Drive and send a link instead. Recipients can receive emails up to 50 MB.
Why does my email attachment exceed the size limit? +
Base64 encoding adds 33% overhead to all attachments. A 15 MB PDF becomes 20 MB in the email. Multiple attachments each add 33% overhead independently. An HTML-heavy email body also adds to total size. Compress files before attaching or use cloud sharing.
How can I send files larger than 25 MB? +
Options: Google Drive (share link, free 15 GB storage), WeTransfer (free 2 GB per transfer), Dropbox (share link), OneDrive (Microsoft), iCloud Drive (Apple). For business: SFTP, SharePoint, Box, or Slack file sharing. These are faster and more reliable than email for large files.
Does image compression reduce email size? +
Yes. JPEG images can be compressed significantly (80% quality vs 100% quality saves 50-70% size with minimal visual difference). Tools: Squoosh.app (free, browser-based), TinyPNG, Photoshop Save for Web. Always compress images before emailing — especially photos from modern smartphones (5-15 MB each).
What is the email attachment limit for Outlook? +
Outlook.com (personal): 20 MB per message. Microsoft 365 Business Basic/Standard: 20-150 MB depending on admin settings. Default Exchange Online limit is 25 MB but admins can raise it to 150 MB. Many corporate IT departments keep it at 10-25 MB for security.
How many photos can I attach to one email? +
Depends on photo file size and email provider limit. Modern smartphone photos: 3-8 MB each. At Gmail 25 MB limit (allowing for Base64 overhead): safely attach 2-3 original photos. Compressed to 1 MB each: attach 15-18 photos. Better option: share a Google Photos or iCloud album link.
