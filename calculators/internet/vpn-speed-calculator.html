VPN Speed Calculator – Overhead, Throughput and Protocol

Calculate your expected VPN speed based on your connection speed, VPN protocol overhead, encryption type, and server distance. Understand why VPNs slow down connections and find the optimal protocol for your use case — speed vs security tradeoff.

VPN services add encryption overhead and routing through an additional server, always reducing speed to some degree. The performance impact ranges from 5% (WireGuard on a nearby server) to 50%+ (OpenVPN on a distant server). Choosing the right protocol and server location minimizes speed loss.

Formula

VPN Throughput = Base Speed × (1 - Protocol Overhead) × Distance Factor | WireGuard overhead: ~5-10% | OpenVPN: ~20-30%

VPN Protocol Speed Comparison

VPN protocols differ significantly in speed, security, and compatibility. Modern protocols like WireGuard achieve near-native speeds due to lightweight design and kernel-level implementation. Older protocols like OpenVPN have significant CPU overhead from their encryption implementation. Choosing the right protocol is the biggest factor in VPN performance.

Server Location Impact

Connecting to a VPN server adds the latency to that server to every request. VPN server in same city: +5-15ms. VPN server in same country: +15-40ms. VPN server on different continent: +100-300ms. For streaming and gaming, always choose a VPN server as close to your physical location as possible unless you specifically need to appear in another country.

When VPN Can Actually Increase Speed

In rare cases, a VPN can improve speeds: if your ISP throttles specific traffic types (Netflix, YouTube, torrents), a VPN hides the traffic type, bypassing throttling. Some ISPs route international traffic through congested paths — a VPN may route through better-peered networks. Test with and without VPN for specific destinations to see if this applies to you.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much speed do I lose using a VPN? +
Speed loss depends on protocol and server distance. WireGuard on a nearby server: 5-15% speed loss. OpenVPN on a nearby server: 15-30% loss. Any protocol on a distant server: 30-60%+ loss. On a fast connection (1 Gbps), WireGuard to a local server may show 900+ Mbps.
Which VPN protocol is fastest? +
WireGuard is the fastest modern VPN protocol, with 5-10% overhead compared to 20-35% for OpenVPN. NordLynx (NordVPN), Lightway (ExpressVPN), and Hydra (Hotspot Shield) are proprietary protocols also achieving near-WireGuard speeds. IKEv2 is a good built-in alternative on iOS and macOS.
Does a VPN affect gaming ping? +
Yes — connecting through a VPN server adds the latency to that server to your ping. A VPN server 50ms away adds ~50ms to all gaming latency. However, if your ISP routes game traffic inefficiently, a gaming VPN (Exitlag, Mudfish) that routes directly to game servers can sometimes reduce ping by 20-100ms on specific routes.
Can a VPN help with ISP throttling? +
Yes. If your ISP throttles specific services (Netflix, YouTube, P2P/BitTorrent), a VPN encrypts all traffic, hiding the type from your ISP. This prevents protocol-based throttling. If your ISP throttles all traffic after a data cap, a VPN will not help since total data usage is still visible.
What is the best VPN for streaming? +
For streaming: prioritize VPNs with fast servers near your target country and no bandwidth limits. NordVPN, ExpressVPN, and Surfshark reliably unblock Netflix, BBC iPlayer, and Disney+. Server load and distance to VPN server affect streaming quality more than the protocol for HD streaming.
Does a VPN reduce upload speed too? +
Yes, VPN overhead affects both upload and download speeds equally (or sometimes upload more on CPU-limited devices). For activities requiring high upload speeds (video streaming to Twitch, cloud backups, large file uploads), choose WireGuard protocol and a nearby server to minimize upload speed loss.
