Geographic Coordinate System

Map Projections

All flat maps distort Earth because a sphere cannot be flattened without distortion. Mercator projection preserves shape but distorts area near poles. Equal-area projections preserve area but distort shape. Web Mercator (Google Maps, OpenStreetMap) is standard for web mapping. For accurate distance and area calculations, always use geographic coordinates rather than measured map distances.

WGS84 — The Global Standard

All GPS devices, Google Maps, and digital mapping systems use the WGS84 (World Geodetic System 1984) coordinate datum. WGS84 models Earth as an ellipsoid with equatorial radius 6,378,137 m and polar radius 6,356,752 m. The Haversine formula used in these calculators assumes a perfect sphere of radius 6,371 km — accurate to within 0.5% for all practical geographic distance calculations.

How Geographic Calculations Work

The calculations in these geography tools are based on fundamental mathematical models of Earth's shape, coordinate system, and spatial relationships. Understanding the underlying principles helps you apply results correctly and know when more precise methods might be needed.

Spherical Earth vs Ellipsoid

Earth is not a perfect sphere but an oblate spheroid — slightly flattened at the poles with an equatorial radius of 6,378,137 m and polar radius of 6,356,752 m. The difference (about 21 km) is small enough that treating Earth as a sphere introduces only about 0.3% error for most geographic calculations. The Haversine formula uses a mean radius of 6,371 km. For precision surveying requiring millimeter accuracy, the Vincenty formula using the full ellipsoid model is required.

Coordinate Reference Systems

Geographic coordinates must be specified in a coordinate reference system (CRS) or datum. The modern global standard is WGS84 — used by GPS satellites, Google Maps, OpenStreetMap, and virtually all digital mapping. Older maps may use local datums (NAD27 in North America, OSGB36 in UK, ED50 in Europe) that differ from WGS84 by up to 200 meters. Always verify the datum when working with precise survey coordinates or mixing data from different sources.

Time Zone Complexity

While Earth's 24-hour day suggests 24 simple time zones each 15 degrees wide, political and geographic factors create significant complexity. The world actually has 38 distinct time zone offsets — some countries use 30-minute or even 45-minute offsets from UTC. China spans 5 geographic time zones but uses a single national time (UTC+8). Russia spans 11 time zones. Indiana (USA) historically had different rules in different counties. Daylight saving time transitions create two periods per year when the effective offset between any two locations may temporarily change.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is percent grade? +
Grade%=(Rise/Run)×100. Rise=elevation change, Run=horizontal distance. 5% grade: 5m rise per 100m run. 100% grade=45°. Roads typically stay below 15-20%.
Maximum road grade? +
US Interstate: 6% max (4% preferred). Urban roads: 10-12%. Rural: 15%. Private driveways: 20-25%. Railroads: 2.5% standard. Steepest public road: Baldwin Street, Dunedin NZ at 35%.
ADA ramp slope? +
Max 8.33% (1:12 ratio). 1 inch rise per 12 inches run. Preferred for long ramps: 5% (1:20). Cross-slope max 2%. Landings required every 30 inches of rise.
Grade to degrees? +
Angle=arctan(grade/100). 5%: arctan(0.05)=2.86°. 10%=5.71°. 45°=100% grade. Vertical wall=infinite percent.
Too steep to drive? +
Above 25-30%: most cars slip on dry pavement. Above 15%: careful driving in wet. Ice and snow: even 5-10% can be impassable.
Minimum grade for drainage? +
Paved: 0.5% minimum. Parking lots: 2%. Gravel: 2%. Lawns: 1-2%. Even 0.2% moves water on smooth pavement.
