Swimming Pace Explained

In swimming, pace is measured per 100 meters (in metric pools) or per 100 yards (in US yards pools). Unlike running where pace per mile is standard, swimmers use shorter intervals due to the higher effort required per distance.

Pace per 100m = Total Time (seconds) ÷ Distance (meters) × 100
Total Time = (Pace per 100m ÷ 100) × Distance in meters

A 25-meter pool requires 40 laps to complete 1,000m (1km). A 50-meter Olympic pool requires 20 laps for the same distance.

What is a good swim pace for beginners? +
A pace of 2:30-3:00 per 100m is typical for recreational beginners. Focus on technique over speed — proper breathing and stroke mechanics will naturally improve your pace more than effort alone.
How many laps is a mile in a pool? +
In a 25-yard pool: 66 laps (1,650 yards ≈ 1 mile). In a 25-meter pool: 64 laps (1,600m ≈ 1 mile). In a 50-meter Olympic pool: 32 laps. A "swim mile" in competition is typically 1,650 yards or 1,500m.
How can I swim faster? +
Key improvements: work on streamlined body position (reduce drag), master bilateral breathing, improve your kick (many recreational swimmers barely kick), practice flip turns, and add interval training — sprint sets at 85-95% effort with rest are more effective than continuous slow laps.
Is swimming good for weight loss? +
Yes. Swimming burns 400-700+ calories per hour and is easy on joints, making it sustainable long-term. The cool water also reduces the heat discomfort that limits intensity in land-based exercise. Combined with diet, swimming is highly effective for fat loss.
