What is VO2 Max?

VO2 max (maximal oxygen uptake) is the maximum rate at which your body can consume oxygen during intense exercise. It is expressed in milliliters of oxygen per kilogram of body weight per minute (mL/kg/min).

A higher VO2 max means your cardiovascular system can deliver more oxygen to working muscles, allowing you to sustain higher intensities for longer. It is the single best predictor of aerobic endurance performance.

What is VO2 max and why does it matter? +
VO2 max measures your body's ability to use oxygen during exercise. Higher values are associated with better cardiovascular health, lower disease risk, and longer lifespan. It is also the main performance predictor for endurance sports.
Can I improve my VO2 max? +
Yes. VO2 max responds strongly to aerobic training. High-intensity interval training (HIIT) and long slow distance runs both improve it. Most untrained people can increase their VO2 max by 15–25% with consistent training.
How accurate is this calculator? +
Field-based estimates have a margin of error of ±3–5 mL/kg/min. Lab testing with a metabolic cart is the most accurate method but expensive. Our estimates are useful for tracking progress and comparing to norms.
Does VO2 max decrease with age? +
Yes, VO2 max naturally declines about 1% per year after age 25 in sedentary individuals. However, regular aerobic exercise can significantly slow this decline and keep your VO2 max comparable to someone 10–20 years younger.
