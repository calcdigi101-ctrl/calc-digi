Sleep Calculator

Find the optimal times to wake up or go to sleep based on your body's natural 90-minute sleep cycles. Waking at the end of a complete cycle means you wake up refreshed — not groggy.

How Sleep Cycles Work

Sleep is not a single uniform state — it cycles through different stages every ~90 minutes. Each complete cycle includes:

  • Stage 1 (N1): Light sleep — the transition from wakefulness (1–7 minutes)
  • Stage 2 (N2): Core sleep — body temperature drops, heart rate slows (10–25 minutes)
  • Stage 3 (N3): Deep sleep — most restorative stage, critical for physical recovery
  • REM Sleep: Rapid Eye Movement — dreaming, memory consolidation, emotional processing

Waking up in the middle of a deep sleep or REM cycle causes sleep inertia — that groggy, disoriented feeling. This calculator helps you avoid it by timing wake-up to the end of a full cycle.

Best Wake Time = Bedtime + (N × 90 minutes) + 15 minutes (to fall asleep)

Tips for Better Sleep Quality

  • Consistent schedule: Go to bed and wake up at the same time every day, even weekends
  • Dark, cool room: 65–68°F (18–20°C) is the optimal sleep temperature
  • No screens 30–60 minutes before bed: Blue light suppresses melatonin production
  • Avoid caffeine after 2 PM: Caffeine has a 5–6 hour half-life in the body
  • Limit alcohol: Alcohol disrupts REM sleep even if it helps you fall asleep faster
  • Exercise regularly: But not within 2–3 hours of bedtime

How many sleep cycles do I need? +
Most adults need 5–6 complete sleep cycles per night (7.5–9 hours). Each cycle is approximately 90 minutes. Fewer than 4 cycles consistently leads to sleep deprivation effects on cognition and health.
Why do I feel tired even after 8 hours of sleep? +
You may be waking in the middle of a deep sleep cycle. This calculator helps you time your sleep so you always wake at the end of a cycle. Other causes include sleep apnea, poor sleep quality, or underlying health conditions.
What time should I go to bed if I wake at 6 AM? +
To complete 5 full cycles (7.5 hours) with 15 minutes to fall asleep, you should be in bed by 10:15 PM. For 6 cycles (9 hours), go to bed by 8:45 PM.
Is a 90-minute nap good? +
Yes! A 90-minute nap completes one full sleep cycle, including REM sleep, which supports creativity and memory. Shorter power naps of 20 minutes avoid deep sleep and prevent grogginess.
