Calorie Burn Calculator

Find out exactly how many calories you burn during any physical activity. Based on MET (Metabolic Equivalent of Task) values from the Compendium of Physical Activities — the gold standard used by researchers and fitness professionals worldwide.

How Calories Burned is Calculated

The calorie burn formula uses MET values — standardized measurements of how much energy an activity requires relative to rest. A MET of 1.0 equals the energy used sitting quietly. Running at 6 mph has a MET of approximately 9.8.

Calories Burned = MET × Weight (kg) × Duration (hours)

For example, a 160 lb (72.7 kg) person cycling at moderate pace (MET 8.0) for 45 minutes burns: 8.0 × 72.7 × 0.75 = 436 calories.

Note: These calculations estimate gross calories burned (total including resting metabolic rate). Net calories (extra calories beyond rest) are slightly lower.

Tips to Burn More Calories

  • Increase intensity: Higher intensity = higher MET = more calories. Add intervals to any cardio workout.
  • Add resistance training: Muscle burns more calories at rest, increasing your baseline metabolic rate.
  • Stay consistent: Daily activity adds up more than occasional intense sessions.
  • NEAT matters: Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis (fidgeting, walking, standing) can account for 200-800 extra calories per day.
  • After-burn effect (EPOC): High-intensity exercise causes elevated calorie burn for hours after the workout ends.

How many calories do I burn running a mile? +
A 155-pound person burns approximately 100 calories per mile running. Weight is the biggest factor — heavier people burn more. Speed affects time but not total calories much, since faster runners cover the same distance in less time.
What exercise burns the most calories? +
Running, jump rope, and rowing burn the most calories per hour (600-900+). But the best exercise is one you can sustain consistently. A 30-minute daily walk beats an occasional sprint session.
How accurate are calorie burn calculators? +
MET-based calculators are accurate within 10-20% for most people. Individual factors like fitness level, body composition, and efficiency affect results. Use them as estimates, not exact measurements. Fitness trackers have similar accuracy.
Do I need to burn 3,500 calories to lose a pound? +
The 3,500 calorie = 1 lb rule is a rough estimate. In practice, weight loss is more complex due to metabolic adaptation and water retention. A 500 calorie/day deficit typically produces about 0.5-1 lb of fat loss per week.
Does cardio or weights burn more calories? +
Cardio burns more calories during the session. However, weight training builds muscle, which increases resting metabolic rate, so you burn more calories 24/7. A combination of both is optimal for long-term weight management.
