Understanding Blood Pressure Numbers

Blood pressure is measured in millimeters of mercury (mmHg) and expressed as two numbers:

  • Systolic (top number): Pressure in your arteries when your heart beats and pumps blood
  • Diastolic (bottom number): Pressure when your heart rests between beats
Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) = (Systolic + 2 × Diastolic) ÷ 3
Pulse Pressure = Systolic − Diastolic

A MAP above 70 mmHg is needed to perfuse vital organs. Pulse pressure above 60 mmHg may indicate arterial stiffness.

How to Get an Accurate Reading

  • Sit quietly for 5 minutes before measuring
  • Keep your back supported and feet flat on the floor
  • Rest your arm at heart level on a flat surface
  • Do not talk during the measurement
  • Avoid caffeine, exercise, and smoking 30 minutes before
  • Take 2-3 readings and use the average
  • Measure at the same time each day for tracking

What is considered normal blood pressure? +
According to the AHA, normal blood pressure is less than 120/80 mmHg. Anything between 120-129 systolic (with diastolic below 80) is considered elevated — not yet hypertension, but a signal to make lifestyle changes.
How can I lower my blood pressure naturally? +
Key lifestyle changes: reduce sodium intake (under 2,300 mg/day), exercise 150+ min/week, maintain healthy weight, limit alcohol, quit smoking, manage stress, and follow the DASH diet (rich in fruits, vegetables, and low-fat dairy).
What causes high blood pressure? +
Primary hypertension (most common) has no single cause — it develops over time due to age, genetics, obesity, sedentary lifestyle, high sodium diet, excessive alcohol, and chronic stress. Secondary hypertension is caused by an underlying condition like kidney disease or thyroid problems.
How often should I check my blood pressure? +
If normal, check at least once a year during a doctor visit. If elevated or hypertensive, monitor at home daily at the same time. Keep a log to share with your doctor. Home monitors are widely available and accurate.
