Cycling Speed, Distance & Time

The basic cycling calculation uses the relationship between speed, distance, and time. Enter any two values to calculate the third.

Speed (mph) = Distance (miles) ÷ Time (hours)
Calories = MET × Weight (kg) × Time (hours)

MET values for cycling: Leisurely (<10 mph) = 4.0, Light (10-12 mph) = 6.0, Moderate (12-14 mph) = 8.0, Vigorous (14-16 mph) = 10.0, Racing (16+ mph) = 12.0

How many calories does cycling burn per hour? +
A 155-pound person burns approximately 420-840+ calories per hour depending on speed. Heavier riders and higher speeds burn more. Hill climbing significantly increases calorie burn compared to flat terrain at the same speed.
What is a good average cycling speed? +
Recreational cyclists average 10-14 mph. Fit cyclists manage 15-18 mph. Competitive cyclists race at 20-25 mph. These are rough guidelines — terrain, wind, elevation, and bike type all significantly affect achievable speeds.
Is cycling or running better for weight loss? +
Running burns slightly more calories per hour at moderate intensity. However, cycling is lower impact, so you can often sustain it longer. Total calorie burn depends on duration and intensity. Both are excellent — the best exercise is the one you will do consistently.
How do I improve my cycling speed? +
Key strategies: interval training (sprint efforts 30-60 seconds), cadence work (aim for 80-100 RPM), strength training (especially squats and lunges), proper bike fit, and consistent base mileage. Improving aerodynamics (lower position) also significantly boosts speed.
