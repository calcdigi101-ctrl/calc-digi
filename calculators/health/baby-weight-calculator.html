Understanding Baby Growth Percentiles

Baby growth percentiles compare your child's measurements to a representative sample of babies of the same age and sex. They are based on CDC (Centers for Disease Control) and WHO growth charts used by pediatricians across the United States.

A percentile does not indicate whether your baby is healthy or unhealthy in isolation. What matters most is consistent growth along any curve. A baby consistently in the 10th percentile is growing normally — a sudden drop from the 70th to 20th percentile may need investigation.

Percentile = Position of baby's measurement relative to 100 babies of same age/sex

What is a normal baby weight percentile? +
Any percentile between the 5th and 95th is generally considered within the normal range by pediatricians. What matters most is that your baby follows a consistent growth curve over time, not the specific percentile number.
My baby is in the 10th percentile — should I be worried? +
Not necessarily. Small babies can be perfectly healthy. Concern arises when a baby drops significantly across percentile lines (e.g., from 50th to 10th) or falls below the 3rd percentile. Always discuss growth concerns with your pediatrician.
How much weight should a newborn gain per week? +
Newborns typically lose 5-10% of birth weight in the first week, then gain about 5-7 oz (140-200g) per week for the first few months. By 4-5 months, most babies double their birth weight.
When should I be concerned about my baby's growth? +
Consult your pediatrician if: your baby drops two or more percentile categories, is consistently below the 3rd percentile, seems uninterested in feeding, appears lethargic, or is not meeting developmental milestones alongside growth.
