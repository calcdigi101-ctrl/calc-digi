Child Height Predictor

Predict your child's adult height using the clinically validated mid-parental height formula used by pediatricians worldwide. Simply enter both parents' heights to get an accurate height range prediction.

How Child Height is Predicted

The most widely used clinical method for predicting adult height is the Mid-Parental Height (MPH) formula, developed and validated through decades of pediatric research. It accounts for the genetic contribution of both parents.

Boy's Predicted Height = (Father's Height + Mother's Height + 5 inches) ÷ 2
Girl's Predicted Height = (Father's Height + Mother's Height − 5 inches) ÷ 2

The prediction comes with a ±2 inch range (±4 cm), meaning 95% of children will fall within 2 inches above or below this predicted height. For example, a boy predicted at 5'10" will likely be between 5'8" and 6'0".

Factors That Influence Child Height

  • Genetics (60-80%): The biggest factor — parental height strongly predicts child height
  • Nutrition: Adequate protein, calcium, vitamin D, and zinc are critical for growth
  • Sleep: Growth hormone is primarily released during deep sleep — 9-11 hours for children
  • Physical activity: Weight-bearing exercise stimulates bone development
  • Chronic illness: Conditions like celiac disease, thyroid issues, or growth hormone deficiency can stunt growth
  • Birth order: First-born children tend to be slightly shorter than later-born siblings

How accurate is this height predictor? +
The mid-parental formula predicts adult height within 2 inches for about 95% of children. It is the same formula used by pediatric endocrinologists. Genetics explains 60-80% of height; nutrition and health during growth years account for the rest.
At what age do children stop growing? +
Girls typically stop growing 2-3 years after their first menstrual period, usually by age 14-15. Boys continue growing until 17-18. Growth plates close once final height is reached, after which height cannot naturally increase.
Can nutrition affect my child's height? +
Yes, significantly. Adequate protein, calcium, vitamin D, and overall caloric intake are essential during growth years. Chronic malnutrition can reduce height by 2-4 inches below genetic potential. Good nutrition helps children reach their full genetic height potential.
Does sleep affect height growth? +
Yes. About 75% of growth hormone is released during deep sleep. Children aged 6-12 need 9-12 hours of sleep, and teenagers need 8-10 hours. Chronic sleep deprivation during growth years can impair growth hormone release and reduce final height.
