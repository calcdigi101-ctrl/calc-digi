What is a Standard Drink?

In the United States, a standard drink contains approximately 14 grams of pure alcohol. Different beverages contain varying amounts of alcohol by volume (ABV), so drink sizes differ:

🥃

1.5 oz · 40% ABV
🍻
Craft Beer
12 oz · 7-9% ABV
BAC = (Alcohol in grams × 5.14) ÷ (Weight in lbs × Gender constant) − (0.015 × Hours drinking)

Gender constant: Male = 0.73, Female = 0.66. Women typically have higher BAC from the same drinks due to differences in body water percentage and alcohol metabolism enzymes.

What is one standard drink in the US? +
One standard US drink contains 14 grams of pure alcohol: 12 oz of regular beer (5%), 5 oz of wine (12%), or 1.5 oz of spirits (40%). Many craft beers and cocktails contain 2 or more standard drinks.
How long does alcohol stay in your system? +
The liver processes about 0.015% BAC per hour — roughly one standard drink per hour. At 0.08% BAC it takes about 5-6 hours to fully sober up. Coffee, water, and food do not speed this process.
Is it safe to drive at 0.07% BAC? +
No. While below the legal limit of 0.08%, driving at 0.07% still significantly impairs reaction time and judgment. Research shows impairment begins at 0.02%. Never drive after drinking — call a rideshare or designate a driver.
Why does BAC differ between men and women? +
Women generally have a higher BAC from the same drinks due to lower body water content (so alcohol is more concentrated) and lower levels of alcohol dehydrogenase enzyme in the stomach, which breaks down alcohol before it enters the bloodstream.
