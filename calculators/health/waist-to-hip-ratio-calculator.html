Waist to Hip Ratio Calculator

Calculate your Waist-to-Hip Ratio (WHR) — a powerful predictor of cardiovascular disease and metabolic syndrome risk. Used by the World Health Organization as a key body composition screening tool.

What is Waist-to-Hip Ratio?

Your waist-to-hip ratio measures the distribution of body fat. People who carry more fat around their abdomen (apple shape) have higher health risks than those who carry fat around their hips and thighs (pear shape).

Visceral fat — fat stored around internal organs in the abdomen — is metabolically active and associated with insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes, heart disease, and certain cancers.

WHR = Waist Circumference ÷ Hip Circumference

How to measure correctly:

  • Waist: Measure at the narrowest point of your torso, usually just above the belly button. Breathe out normally before measuring.
  • Hips: Measure at the widest point of your hips and buttocks.

What is a healthy waist to hip ratio? +
The WHO defines healthy WHR as below 0.85 for women and below 0.90 for men. Values above these thresholds indicate increased abdominal fat and elevated health risk.
Is WHR a better health indicator than BMI? +
For predicting cardiovascular and metabolic disease risk, WHR is generally considered superior to BMI. BMI does not account for fat distribution or muscle mass, while WHR directly measures dangerous abdominal fat accumulation.
How can I reduce my waist to hip ratio? +
Spot reduction is not possible, but overall fat loss reduces abdominal fat effectively. Combine aerobic exercise (150+ min/week), resistance training, and a moderate calorie deficit. Reducing refined carbs and added sugars specifically helps reduce visceral fat.
Does WHR change with age? +
Yes. As people age, fat distribution tends to shift toward the abdomen, increasing WHR. This is especially common after menopause in women due to hormonal changes. Maintaining activity levels and muscle mass helps counteract this trend.
