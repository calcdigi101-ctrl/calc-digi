1RM Formulas Explained

A one rep max is the maximum weight you can lift for a single repetition with proper form. Directly testing it carries injury risk, so we use validated formulas from a submaximal effort:

Epley: 1RM = Weight × (1 + Reps ÷ 30)
Brzycki: 1RM = Weight × 36 ÷ (37 − Reps)
Lander: 1RM = (100 × Weight) ÷ (101.3 − 2.67123 × Reps)

Our calculator uses the average of all three for the most accurate estimate. Best results are obtained using a weight you can lift for 3–10 reps — beyond 10 reps, accuracy drops.

How accurate are 1RM calculators? +
1RM formulas are most accurate when using 1–5 reps. The accuracy decreases at higher rep ranges (10+) because muscular endurance becomes a larger factor. For 3–6 rep sets, expect within 5–10% of your true max.
What rep range should I use to estimate 1RM? +
Use a weight you can lift for 3–8 reps with maximum effort. This gives the most accurate estimate. Using a very light weight for 15+ reps reduces accuracy significantly.
What is a good bench press 1RM for my weight? +
General benchmarks for men: Beginner = 0.5× bodyweight, Intermediate = 1.0×, Advanced = 1.5×, Elite = 2.0+×. For women these are roughly 20–30% lower. These vary by age, training history, and body composition.
Should I actually try to lift my calculated 1RM? +
Not unless you are an experienced lifter with proper technique and a spotter. Use the calculated 1RM for programming purposes only. True max attempts carry significant injury risk without proper preparation.
