How Many Calories Do You Need?

Your calorie needs are determined by your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) — the calories your body burns at rest — multiplied by your activity level. This gives your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE).

To lose weight, eat fewer calories than your TDEE. To gain weight, eat more. To maintain, match your TDEE.

TDEE = BMR × Activity Multiplier

BMR (male) = (10 × kg) + (6.25 × cm) – (5 × age) + 5
BMR (female) = (10 × kg) + (6.25 × cm) – (5 × age) – 161

Activity Level Guide

  • Sedentary: Little or no exercise, desk job (×1.2)
  • Lightly Active: Light exercise 1–3 days/week (×1.375)
  • Moderately Active: Moderate exercise 3–5 days/week (×1.55)
  • Very Active: Hard exercise 6–7 days/week (×1.725)
  • Extra Active: Very hard exercise, physical job (×1.9)

How many calories should I eat per day? +
The average adult needs 1,600–3,000 calories per day depending on age, sex, height, weight, and activity level. Use our calculator above to get your personalized recommendation.
How many calories do I need to lose 1 pound per week? +
To lose 1 lb per week, you need a 500-calorie daily deficit below your TDEE. For 2 lbs per week, a 1,000-calorie deficit is needed. Never go below 1,200 calories (women) or 1,500 calories (men) without medical supervision.
What is the difference between BMR and TDEE? +
BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is how many calories your body burns at complete rest. TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) is your BMR multiplied by your activity level — it's the total calories you burn in a day.
Is 1200 calories a day enough? +
1,200 calories is the generally accepted minimum for women, but it may be too low for most adults and can lead to nutrient deficiencies. Aim for a moderate deficit (300–500 calories below TDEE) for sustainable, healthy weight loss.
