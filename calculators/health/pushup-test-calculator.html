How to Do a Perfect Pushup

  • Hand position: Slightly wider than shoulder-width, fingers pointing forward
  • Body alignment: Straight line from head to heels — no sagging hips or piked butt
  • Elbow angle: 45 degrees from your torso, not flared out to 90 degrees
  • Range of motion: Chest nearly touches the ground at the bottom; arms fully extended at the top
  • Breathing: Inhale on the way down, exhale as you push up
  • Core engagement: Brace your core throughout — treat it like a moving plank

How many pushups should I be able to do at my age? +
Standards vary by age and sex. For men in their 20s, 22+ is considered good. For women in their 20s, 18+ is good. Use the tables above to find your specific age group standard. Progress matters more than comparison.
How can I increase my pushup count quickly? +
Do pushups 3-4 times per week with rest days between. Use the "greasing the groove" method — several submaximal sets throughout the day. Add negative pushups (slow 3-5 second lowering) to build strength faster. Progress by adding 1-2 reps per set each week.
Are knee pushups useful? +
Knee pushups are a valid progression for beginners who cannot do standard pushups yet. They train the same pushing muscles with less resistance. However, transition to full pushups as soon as possible, as knee pushups do not build functional core strength.
What muscles do pushups work? +
Pushups primarily work the pectorals (chest), triceps, and anterior deltoids (front shoulders). They also engage the core, serratus anterior (rib muscles), and lower back as stabilizers. They are one of the most efficient upper-body exercises.
