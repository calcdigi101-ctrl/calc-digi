BMI Formula

BMI uses your weight and height to produce a single number. The formula used in the United States (Imperial units):

BMI = (Weight in lbs × 703) ÷ (Height in inches)²

For example, someone who is 5'9" (69 inches) and weighs 170 lbs would have a BMI of: (170 × 703) ÷ (69²) = 119,510 ÷ 4,761 ≈ 25.1

What is a healthy BMI range for adults? +
According to the CDC, a healthy BMI is between 18.5 and 24.9 for adults. Below 18.5 is underweight, 25–29.9 is overweight, and 30 or above is considered obese.
How accurate is BMI as a health measure? +
BMI is a useful population-level screening tool but is not a perfect measure of individual health. It does not distinguish between muscle and fat, so athletes and very muscular individuals may have a high BMI despite low body fat. Always consult your doctor for a full assessment.
Is the BMI calculation different for men and women? +
The formula is identical for men and women. However, women naturally carry more body fat than men at the same BMI, so some doctors consider context when interpreting results.
What BMI is considered obese? +
A BMI of 30 or higher is classified as obese by the CDC and WHO. Class 1 obesity is 30–34.9, Class 2 is 35–39.9, and Class 3 (severe obesity) is 40 or above. If you fall in these ranges, speaking with a healthcare provider is strongly recommended.
What is a good BMI for my age? +
For adults 20 and older, the same BMI ranges apply regardless of age. For children and teens (2–19), BMI is interpreted using age- and sex-specific percentiles. This calculator is for adults only.
