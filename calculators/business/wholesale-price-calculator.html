Wholesale Price Calculator – Retail Price and Reseller Margins

Calculate wholesale pricing from retail price and desired margins, find the retail price from wholesale cost, or determine the margin a reseller makes at any given wholesale price. Essential for manufacturers setting wholesale prices and retailers evaluating supplier pricing.

Wholesale pricing must balance manufacturer profitability with enough margin for distributors and retailers to profit. A typical wholesale chain: manufacturer sells at 50% of retail (50% margin for retailer), or manufacturer → distributor (30% margin) → retailer (50% margin). Understanding the full pricing chain helps set competitive wholesale prices.

Formula

Wholesale Price = Retail Price × (1 - Retailer Margin%) | Retailer Margin = (Retail - Wholesale) / Retail × 100 | Manufacturer Margin = (Wholesale - COGS) / Wholesale × 100

Understanding the Wholesale Pricing Chain

Products typically pass through multiple pricing tiers from manufacturer to end consumer. Each tier adds margin to cover costs and profit. Understanding the full chain helps manufacturers price competitively while maintaining profitability at every level.

Standard Wholesale Discount from Retail

Common wholesale pricing conventions: Keystone wholesale (most retail): wholesale = 50% of retail price. MAP (Minimum Advertised Price): manufacturer sets floor price retailers can advertise. MSRP (Manufacturer Suggested Retail Price): suggested but not mandatory in most countries. Standard wholesale discount for different channels: small retail boutiques 50% off retail, department stores 55-60% off retail, large chain retailers 60-65% off retail.

Tiered Wholesale Pricing

Many manufacturers offer tiered pricing based on order quantity: minimum order 12 units at $18 each (standard wholesale), 50+ units at $16 each (volume discount), 200+ units at $14 each (preferred pricing). Tiered pricing rewards larger buyers, moves more inventory per order, and reduces per-transaction costs. Set tiers based on your production economics and preferred customer profiles.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a typical wholesale to retail markup? +
The standard retail markup on wholesale price is 100% (keystone pricing) — doubling the wholesale cost. A product wholesaling for $25 retails for $50. This gives retailers a 50% gross margin. Luxury goods: 200-400% retail markup. Electronics: 10-30% retail markup. Grocery: 15-50%. The markup depends on category competition, perceived value, and channel costs.
How do I calculate my wholesale price? +
Method 1 (cost-based): Wholesale = COGS / (1 - desired margin). At 60% manufacturer margin on $10 COGS: $10 / (1-0.60) = $25 wholesale. Method 2 (retail-based): Wholesale = Retail × (1 - retailer margin%). If retail is $50 and retailers need 50% margin: $50 × (1-0.50) = $25 wholesale.
What is MAP pricing and why does it matter? +
MAP (Minimum Advertised Price) is the lowest price a retailer can publicly advertise a product. Manufacturers set MAP to protect brand value and prevent channel conflict (online resellers undercutting brick-and-mortar). MAP violations can result in losing wholesale account access. MAP does not restrict actual selling price — a retailer can sell below MAP at the register but cannot advertise that price.
What is the difference between wholesale and distribution? +
Wholesale: manufacturer sells directly to retailers in bulk at discounted prices. Distribution: manufacturer sells to distributors who then resell to retailers. Distributors add 20-40% margin and provide services: warehousing, logistics, sales representation, and credit risk management. Distribution makes sense for manufacturers who cannot manage many retail relationships directly.
How much margin do retailers expect on wholesale goods? +
Typical retailer margin expectations by channel: boutique/specialty retail: 50-60% margin (requires 2-2.5× wholesale price). Department stores: 55-60% margin. Mass market (Target, Walmart): 40-50% margin but massive volume. Online retailers: 30-50% margin. Grocery: 25-40%. Always ask potential retail buyers what margin they require before setting wholesale price.
What is a good profit margin for wholesale? +
Manufacturer selling wholesale should target: gross margin on wholesale price of 50-60% (after COGS). This means wholesale price should be 2-2.5× manufacturing cost. After overhead allocation, aim for 20-35% net margin on wholesale revenue. If wholesale margins do not support profitability after overhead, the product needs repricing, cost reduction, or direct-to-consumer channel development.
