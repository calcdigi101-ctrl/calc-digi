Invoice Calculator – Line Items, Tax, Discount and Total Due

Calculate your invoice total by adding line items with quantities and prices, applying discounts, adding sales tax, and finding the final amount due. Essential for freelancers, small businesses, and contractors creating professional invoices.

Professional invoicing requires accurate calculation of subtotals, discounts, taxes, and totals. Errors in invoices damage business credibility and can cause payment delays. This calculator handles multi-line invoices with custom discounts and tax rates for accurate billing every time.

Formula

Subtotal = Sum of (Qty × Unit Price) | After Discount = Subtotal × (1 - Discount%) | Total Due = After Discount × (1 + Tax Rate%)

Professional Invoice Best Practices

A professional invoice includes: unique invoice number, invoice date and due date, your business name and contact information, client name and address, itemized line items with descriptions, quantities, and unit prices, subtotal, discounts applied, taxes charged, total due, payment terms, and accepted payment methods.

Late Payment Fees

US businesses can legally charge late payment fees if disclosed in the original contract or invoice. Common late fees: 1.5% per month (18% annually) is the most common rate. Some states cap late fees at 10-15% annually. Always include payment terms and late fee policy in contracts and on invoices before the first invoice is issued — adding late fees retrospectively may not be enforceable.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should be included on an invoice? +
Required invoice elements: invoice number (unique sequential), invoice date, payment due date, your business name, address, and contact info, client name and billing address, itemized services/products with descriptions, quantities, and rates, subtotal, any discounts, applicable taxes with rate specified, total amount due, payment terms, and payment method instructions.
What are standard invoice payment terms? +
Most common: Net 30 (due within 30 days of invoice date). Small businesses and freelancers often use Net 15 or Due on Receipt. Large enterprises may negotiate Net 60 or Net 90. Early payment discount: 2/10 Net 30 means 2% discount if paid within 10 days, otherwise full amount in 30 days.
How do I calculate invoice tax? +
Sales tax on invoice = Subtotal (after discounts) × Tax Rate. Always apply discount before calculating tax — tax is calculated on the discounted amount. If invoice total is $1,000 with 10% discount and 8.25% tax: After discount = $900. Tax = $900 × 0.0825 = $74.25. Total due = $974.25.
What is a proforma invoice? +
A proforma invoice is a preliminary invoice sent before goods or services are delivered. It shows the expected price and serves as a quotation. It is not an accounting document and does not create a legal obligation. Used for: customs clearance for international shipments, buyer financing applications, and formal project cost confirmations before work begins.
Can I charge late fees on unpaid invoices? +
Yes, if disclosed in your contract or on the invoice before the due date. Common late fee: 1.5% per month (18% per year). Some states cap rates — check your state usury laws. To enforce late fees: include them in every contract, state them on every invoice ("Invoices unpaid after 30 days subject to 1.5% monthly late fee"), and send reminders before the due date.
How do I handle disputed invoices? +
Best practices: clearly document all work with scope, timescopes, and deliverables in contracts. When disputes arise: respond promptly, gather supporting documentation, offer a structured payment plan if the client has genuine hardship, and consider credit for partial completion rather than writing off entirely. Small claims court is available for disputes up to $5,000-15,000 (varies by state) at low cost.
