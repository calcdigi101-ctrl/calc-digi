Payroll Calculator – Net Pay, Withholding and Employer Taxes

Calculate employee net take-home pay after federal income tax withholding, Social Security, Medicare (FICA), and state taxes. Also shows the total employer payroll tax burden for any salary or hourly wage.

Payroll processing requires calculating multiple taxes simultaneously — federal income tax withholding (based on W-4 allowances), FICA (Social Security 6.2% + Medicare 1.45%), state income tax, and any local taxes. This calculator estimates net pay for budgeting and helps employers understand total payroll cost.

Formula

Net Pay = Gross Pay - Federal Tax - FICA (7.65%) - State Tax - Other Deductions | Employer Cost = Gross Pay × (1 + Employer FICA 7.65%)

Frequently Asked Questions

How is payroll tax calculated? +
Employee payroll taxes: FICA = Gross Pay × 7.65% (6.2% SS + 1.45% Medicare). Federal Income Tax: calculated using tax brackets and W-4 allowances. State income tax: varies by state (0-13%). Net Pay = Gross Pay - All Tax Withholding - Benefits Deductions. Employer pays matching FICA (7.65%) plus unemployment taxes.
What is the difference between gross pay and net pay? +
Gross pay: total earnings before any deductions (salary or hours × rate). Net pay (take-home): gross pay minus all taxes and deductions. Typical deductions: federal income tax (10-22% for middle income), FICA (7.65%), state income tax (0-13%), health insurance premium, 401k contribution, and other voluntary deductions. Most employees take home 65-80% of gross pay.
How much does an employer pay in payroll taxes? +
Employer payroll taxes: matching FICA 7.65% (6.2% SS + 1.45% Medicare), FUTA 0.6% (on first $7,000), SUTA varies by state (typically 1-5% on first $7,000-$40,000). Total employer tax burden beyond gross wages: approximately 10-15% of gross wages. This is in addition to benefits, workers comp, and other costs.
What is a W-4 form and how does it affect withholding? +
The W-4 (Employee Withholding Certificate) tells your employer how much federal income tax to withhold from each paycheck. Since 2020, it uses a dollar-based system instead of allowances. Claiming higher deductions/credits reduces withholding (larger paycheck, potential tax bill at filing). Lower claims increase withholding (smaller paycheck, likely refund). Update your W-4 after major life changes.
How do I calculate bi-weekly payroll? +
Bi-weekly gross pay = Annual salary / 26 pay periods. Monthly salary: $5,000/month = $60,000/year = $60,000/26 = $2,307.69 bi-weekly. Bi-weekly FICA = $2,307.69 × 7.65% = $176.54. Federal income tax withholding depends on W-4 and tax brackets. Net pay after FICA only: $2,307.69 - $176.54 = $2,131.15 (before income tax and other deductions).
What states have no income tax? +
Nine states have no state income tax: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire (interest/dividends only), South Dakota, Tennessee (interest/dividends only), Texas, Washington, Wyoming. Washington has no income tax but has a capital gains tax. Moving to a no-income-tax state can significantly increase take-home pay for high earners.
