Profit Margin Calculator – Gross, Net and Operating Margin

Calculate gross profit margin, net profit margin, and operating margin from revenue and cost figures. Also find the required selling price to achieve any target profit margin — essential for pricing strategy and financial analysis.

Profit margin is the percentage of revenue that becomes profit after accounting for costs. Gross margin shows manufacturing efficiency. Operating margin shows operational efficiency. Net margin shows overall profitability. Understanding all three gives a complete picture of business financial health.

Formula

Gross Margin = (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue × 100 | Net Margin = Net Profit / Revenue × 100 | Operating Margin = Operating Income / Revenue × 100

Types of Profit Margin

There are three main profit margin metrics, each telling a different part of the profitability story. Analyzing all three reveals where value is created and where costs erode it — providing specific areas for improvement.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good profit margin for a small business? +
For small businesses, net profit margins of 7-10% are average, 10-20% is above average, and above 20% is excellent. However, benchmarks vary significantly by industry. Retailers often operate on 2-5% net margins. Service businesses can achieve 20-30%. Technology companies 15-25%. Compare your margins to industry averages, not across industries.
What is the difference between gross and net profit margin? +
Gross profit margin = (Revenue - Cost of Goods Sold) / Revenue. Only deducts direct production costs. Net profit margin = Net Income / Revenue. Deducts ALL costs including COGS, operating expenses, interest, and taxes. A business can have a 60% gross margin but only 5% net margin if operating costs are high.
How do I calculate my selling price from a target margin? +
Selling Price = Cost / (1 - Target Margin). Example: cost is $60, target gross margin is 40%: Price = $60 / (1 - 0.40) = $60 / 0.60 = $100. This is different from markup: a 40% margin is NOT the same as 40% markup. Markup is on cost (cost × 1.40 = $84), margin is on price.
Why is my gross margin high but net margin low? +
High gross but low net margin indicates operating expenses are consuming profit. Common causes: high marketing/advertising spend, excessive administrative overhead, high rent, large management team relative to revenue, high interest on debt, or significant depreciation. Review SG&A (Selling, General & Administrative) expenses as a percentage of revenue.
What is the difference between margin and markup? +
Margin is profit as a percentage of selling price. Markup is profit as a percentage of cost. A product costing $50 sold for $100: Margin = $50/$100 = 50%. Markup = $50/$50 = 100%. For the same product, markup % is always higher than margin %. Conversion: Margin = Markup / (1 + Markup). Markup = Margin / (1 - Margin).
How can I improve my profit margins? +
Four levers: 1) Raise prices (test market elasticity — even 5-10% price increase dramatically improves margins if volume holds). 2) Reduce COGS (negotiate with suppliers, improve production efficiency, reduce waste). 3) Cut operating expenses (automate, consolidate vendors, reduce overhead). 4) Shift product mix (promote higher-margin products). Measure the impact of each change on all three margin types.
