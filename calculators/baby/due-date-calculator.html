When to Contact Your Pediatrician

Always consult your pediatrician if your baby is consistently below the 5th percentile for weight or height, losing weight after the first two weeks, feeding fewer than 6-8 times per day in the first month, sleeping significantly more or less than recommended ranges, or not reaching developmental milestones. Pregnancy Timeline – Complete Guide

Pregnancy typically lasts 40 weeks (280 days) from the first day of your last menstrual period (LMP), or about 38 weeks from conception. Naegele's Rule — the standard method — adds 280 days to the LMP date. Only about 4% of babies are born on their exact due date; 80% are born within 2 weeks of the EDD.

Signs of Labor

Contact your healthcare provider when contractions are 5 minutes apart for 1 hour, your water breaks, you have heavy bleeding, or you notice significantly decreased fetal movement. Early labor signs include irregular contractions (Braxton Hicks), mucus plug loss, and the baby dropping lower in the pelvis (lightening). Every pregnancy is different — follow your healthcare provider guidance for when to go to the hospital.

When to Call Your Pediatrician

Contact your pediatrician right away if your baby has a fever (100.4°F or higher in newborns under 3 months), is not feeding well, seems unusually sleepy or difficult to wake, has fewer than 6 wet diapers per day after day 4, is not regaining birth weight by 2 weeks, has a soft spot bulging or sunken, or you have any concern about development or behavior. Trust your instincts — you know your baby best.

Parenting Tips and Resources

Parenting a baby and toddler involves constant learning and adjustment. Every child develops at their own pace within a range of normal variation. The most important thing you can give your child is a secure, loving, and responsive relationship — this is the foundation of healthy development, emotional regulation, and lifelong wellbeing.

Key Parenting Principles

Respond consistently to your baby's cues — this builds trust and secure attachment. Babies cannot be spoiled in the first year. Create predictable routines for feeding, sleep, and play — routines reduce stress for both babies and parents. Talk, read, and sing to your baby from birth — language exposure in the first 3 years dramatically shapes brain development and future literacy. Limit screen time to video chatting only before 18-24 months. Get vaccinations on schedule — the childhood immunization schedule is carefully designed for maximum protection when babies are most vulnerable.

Postpartum Health for Parents

Postpartum depression (PPD) affects about 1 in 8 mothers and 1 in 10 fathers after birth. It is a medical condition, not a personal failure. Symptoms include persistent sadness, anxiety, difficulty bonding, sleep problems beyond newborn disruption, and thoughts of self-harm. PPD is treatable with therapy, medication, or both. Contact your healthcare provider immediately if you or your partner experiences these symptoms. Partners: check in regularly and take an active role in infant care — shared responsibility reduces PPD risk significantly.

Cost of Raising a Child

USDA estimates the cost of raising a child to age 18 at $233,610 for a middle-income family (not including college). Major expenses: housing 29%, food 18%, childcare and education 16%, transportation 15%, healthcare 9%, clothing 6%, miscellaneous 7%. Childcare is the fastest-rising cost — averaging $15,000-35,000 per year in major US cities for infant care. Financial planning early — 529 college savings, life insurance, emergency fund — significantly reduces financial stress during the parenting years.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is due date calculated? +
Due Date = LMP + 280 days (Naegele Rule). Add 1 year, subtract 3 months, add 7 days to the LMP date. If LMP is January 1: due date is October 8. Ultrasound dating (crown-rump length before 14 weeks) is more accurate than LMP calculation.
How accurate is the due date? +
Only 4% of babies are born on their exact due date. About 80% are born within 2 weeks of EDD. Normal delivery range: 37-42 weeks. Before 37 weeks = preterm. After 42 weeks = post-term, monitored closely. Ultrasound in first trimester is most accurate dating method.
What are the three trimesters? +
First trimester: weeks 1-12 (organ formation). Second trimester: weeks 13-27 (rapid growth, movement felt). Third trimester: weeks 28-40 (weight gain, lung maturation). Each trimester brings unique physical changes and developmental milestones for both mother and baby.
When does morning sickness end? +
Most women experience morning sickness between weeks 6-12, peaking around week 9. About 80% see improvement by end of first trimester (week 12-14). For 10-15% it continues into second trimester. Hyperemesis gravidarum (severe vomiting) affects 1-2% and may require medical treatment.
What is a full-term pregnancy? +
37-38 weeks: early term. 39-40 weeks: full term. 41 weeks: late term. 42+ weeks: post-term. Full-term babies (39-40 weeks) have the best outcomes. Elective delivery before 39 weeks without medical reason increases neonatal risks. Post-term pregnancies are typically induced at 41-42 weeks.
When should I call my doctor? +
Immediately: heavy vaginal bleeding, severe abdominal pain, signs of preeclampsia (severe headache, visual changes, sudden swelling), decreased fetal movement after 28 weeks, water breaking. These calculators provide educational information only — always follow your healthcare provider guidance.
