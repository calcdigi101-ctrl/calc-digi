Your GPA (Grade Point Average) is one of the most important metrics in your academic career — affecting college admissions, scholarship eligibility, graduate school applications, and even job prospects. Understanding how GPA is calculated helps you strategically plan your coursework and grade targets.

Formula

GPA = Sum of (Grade Points × Credit Hours) / Total Credit Hours | A=4.0, B=3.0, C=2.0, D=1.0, F=0.0

How GPA is Calculated

GPA (Grade Point Average) is calculated by multiplying each course grade points by the credit hours, summing these values, then dividing by total credit hours. This weighted average means a 3-credit course affects your GPA three times as much as a 1-credit course.

Weighted vs Unweighted GPA

Unweighted GPA: all courses treated equally on 4.0 scale. Weighted GPA: AP, IB, and honors courses get bonus points (typically 0.5 for honors, 1.0 for AP/IB) — allowing a maximum of 5.0. Colleges recalculate GPA using their own methods, so always check each institution preference. Most college applications request unweighted GPA.

What is a Good GPA?

High school: 3.5+ GPA puts you in the top academic tier for most colleges. 3.7+ GPA for competitive universities. 3.9+ for Ivy League consideration. College: 3.0+ GPA maintains good academic standing. 3.5+ GPA for most graduate school programs. 3.7+ for medical, law, or PhD programs. Dean List is typically 3.5+ per semester.

GPA Scale Differences

US colleges primarily use 4.0 scale. Some schools use plus/minus grading (adding A- = 3.7, B+ = 3.3 etc.). International students: UK uses 1st class/2:1/2:2/3rd, converted to 4.0 for US applications. Canadian universities use percentage or letter grades. Always clarify the scale when comparing GPAs across institutions.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate my GPA? +
GPA = Sum of (Grade Points × Credits) / Total Credits. Example: Math (A=4.0) × 3 credits = 12, English (B=3.0) × 3 credits = 9, Biology (A-=3.7) × 4 credits = 14.8. Total points = 35.8, total credits = 10, GPA = 35.8/10 = 3.58.
What is a 3.5 GPA equivalent to? +
A 3.5 GPA is equivalent to a B+ average (87-89% range). It represents strong academic performance — above average but not straight As. A 3.5 GPA makes you competitive for most universities and many scholarships, and maintains eligibility for most merit aid programs.
How do I raise my GPA? +
To raise your GPA: take more credit hours where you can earn As (dilutes lower grades), retake courses where you earned C or below (some schools replace the old grade), focus maximum effort on high-credit courses, attend office hours and tutoring, and develop consistent study habits rather than cramming.
What GPA do I need for graduate school? +
Most master programs require minimum 3.0 GPA. Top programs prefer 3.5+. MBA programs at top schools: 3.5+ with strong GMAT/GRE. Law school (T14): 3.7+. Medical school: 3.7+ science GPA. PhD programs: 3.5+ with strong research experience. Some programs consider GPA trend (improvement) over absolute number.
Does a bad freshman GPA ruin my chances? +
No. Admissions officers and employers often look at GPA trends. Showing significant improvement (2.5 freshman year to 3.7 senior year) demonstrates growth and maturity. Some graduate programs specifically request just the last 60 credit hours of GPA. Explain early poor performance in personal statements if needed.
What is a weighted GPA vs unweighted? +
Unweighted GPA: all courses on 4.0 scale regardless of difficulty. Weighted GPA: AP/IB courses get +1.0 bonus, honors courses +0.5, allowing a 5.0 maximum. A straight-A student in all AP courses has 5.0 weighted but still 4.0 unweighted. Most colleges recalculate GPA their own way.
