Reading Speed Calculator – Words Per Minute and Book Time

Calculate your reading speed in words per minute (WPM) and find out how long it will take you to read any book, article, or study assignment. Improve your reading speed and comprehension with proven techniques.

The average adult reads 200-300 words per minute with good comprehension. Speed readers can achieve 400-700 WPM. Understanding your reading speed helps you realistically schedule reading assignments, plan study time for textbooks, and identify opportunities to improve your reading efficiency.

Formula

Reading Speed (WPM) = Words Read / Time (minutes) | Reading Time = Total Words / WPM | 1 page ≈ 250-300 words

Reading Speed by Level

Reading speed varies significantly by purpose and material. Skimming for key points is much faster than careful analytical reading of complex material. Matching your reading speed to the purpose and material type maximizes both efficiency and comprehension.

How to Measure Your Reading Speed

Method: Select a passage of known word count (or count words in a full page — typically 250-300 words for standard books). Read at your normal comfortable pace for exactly 1 minute. Count the words you read. This is your WPM. Alternatively, read a full passage, note the time, count total words, and calculate WPM = words / minutes.

Speed Reading Techniques

Proven techniques to increase reading speed: eliminate subvocalization (silently saying each word — the #1 limiter of reading speed), use a pointer or finger to guide your eyes and maintain pace, expand your eye fixation span to read 2-3 words per fixation instead of 1, minimize regressions (re-reading), and practice chunking — reading groups of words as a unit.

Speed vs Comprehension Tradeoff

Above 400 WPM, comprehension typically begins to decline. For academic reading requiring deep understanding and retention, 200-300 WPM with active engagement (note-taking, questioning, summarizing) is more effective than speed reading at 600 WPM with poor retention. Reserve speed reading for pleasure reading, news, and familiar material.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average reading speed for adults? +
The average adult reads 200-300 words per minute (WPM) with good comprehension. College students average 250-350 WPM. Professionals who read extensively average 300-400 WPM. Elementary school children: 100-150 WPM. Speed readers can achieve 400-700 WPM with some comprehension tradeoff.
How long does it take to read a 300-page book? +
A 300-page book has approximately 75,000-90,000 words (250-300 words per page). At average 250 WPM: 75,000 / 250 = 300 minutes = 5 hours. At 350 WPM: about 3.6 hours. Harry Potter (77,000 words) at 250 WPM = 5.1 hours. The Bible (780,000 words) at 250 WPM = 52 hours.
How can I increase my reading speed? +
Practice daily reading, eliminate subvocalization (mouthing words), use a pointer to guide your eyes, expand your peripheral vision to read more words per fixation, take a speed reading course (Spreeder, Iris Reading), and time yourself regularly. Consistent practice for 15-20 minutes daily can double reading speed within 3 months.
How many pages can you read in one hour? +
At 250 WPM with 250 words per page: 250 WPM × 60 min = 15,000 words / 250 = 60 pages per hour. At 350 WPM: 84 pages per hour. Academic textbooks with figures and equations: 15-30 pages per hour. Dense philosophical or legal text: 10-20 pages per hour. Fiction at leisure: 50-80+ pages per hour.
What is WPM and why does it matter for students? +
WPM (Words Per Minute) measures reading speed. For students, WPM determines how long assignments take. A professor assigning 80 pages per week (≈20,000 words): at 200 WPM = 100 minutes (1hr 40min). At 350 WPM = 57 minutes. Knowing your WPM allows accurate time planning for reading assignments.
Does reading speed affect comprehension? +
Moderate speed increases (from 200 to 300 WPM) generally do not hurt comprehension when practiced gradually. Comprehension typically begins dropping above 400-500 WPM. For academic material requiring full understanding: read at 200-300 WPM with active engagement (highlighting, note-taking) rather than faster passive reading.
