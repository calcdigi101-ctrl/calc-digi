Car Depreciation Calculator – Annual Value Loss and Resale Value

Calculate how much your vehicle depreciates each year and its current market value based on age and mileage. Understand why new cars lose 20% of value in the first year and how depreciation affects your total cost of vehicle ownership.

Car depreciation is the largest hidden cost of vehicle ownership — often exceeding fuel costs. A new $35,000 car loses approximately $7,000 in the first year alone and about 50% of its value in the first three years. Understanding depreciation helps you make smarter vehicle buying decisions.

Formula

Year 1 Loss = Purchase Price × 20% | Years 2-5 = Remaining Value × 15% per year | 5-Year Value ≈ 40% of Original Price

How Car Depreciation Works

Depreciation is the loss in vehicle value over time and use. New cars depreciate fastest in the first year (15-25% of purchase price) because they instantly become "used." The rate then slows to approximately 15-18% per year for years 2-5. After 5 years, many cars have lost 40-60% of their original value depending on brand, model, and condition.

Best and Worst Depreciation Brands

Best resale value (lowest depreciation): Toyota Tacoma, Toyota 4Runner, Jeep Wrangler, Subaru Outback, Honda Civic and CR-V. These vehicles retain 50-60% of value after 5 years. Worst resale value (fastest depreciation): Luxury sedans (Mercedes E-Class, BMW 5 Series), Cadillac, Lincoln, large domestic sedans. These may retain only 30-35% of value after 5 years.

The True Cost of New vs Used

Buying a 2-3 year old used car lets someone else absorb the steepest depreciation. A $35,000 new car at year 3 is worth approximately $21,000 — you can buy it for $21,000 and likely sell it for $14,000 at year 6 — only $7,000 in depreciation vs $21,000 for the original new car buyer. Used car buyers pay less depreciation per year of ownership.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a car depreciate per year? +
New cars lose 15-25% of value in the first year, then 10-18% per year in subsequent years. After 5 years, most cars are worth 40-50% of their original price. Luxury vehicles depreciate faster (20-30%/yr). Japanese brands (Toyota, Honda) depreciate slowest (10-15%/yr) due to reliability reputation.
What car has the best resale value? +
Consistently best resale value (5-year retention): Toyota Tacoma (60-65%), Jeep Wrangler (55-65%), Toyota 4Runner (55-60%), Honda CR-V (50-55%), Toyota Camry (50-55%). These vehicles maintain value due to strong demand, reliability reputation, and limited supply relative to demand.
How much does a new car lose in value when you drive it off the lot? +
A new car loses approximately 10-20% of its value the moment it is titled (becomes "used"), plus the dealer markup. On a $35,000 car, this can be $3,500-7,000 in immediate value loss. This is the primary reason financial experts often recommend buying 1-3 year old certified pre-owned vehicles.
Does mileage affect car depreciation more than age? +
Both matter, but high mileage significantly accelerates depreciation. A 3-year-old car with 75,000 miles depreciates much faster than one with 30,000 miles. Standard mileage assumption is 12,000-15,000 miles/year — vehicles above this depreciate faster. Vehicles below average mileage for their age command a premium.
What is the best age to buy a used car for value? +
2-4 year old used cars offer the best value balance: the first-owner has absorbed the steepest depreciation (30-40%), the car still has years of reliable life left, warranty coverage may still apply (certified pre-owned), and financing rates are still reasonable. Avoid cars over 100,000 miles unless they are known for reliability (Toyota, Honda).
How does depreciation affect car ownership cost? +
Depreciation is often the largest vehicle ownership cost, exceeding fuel in many cases. On a $35,000 car losing 15% annually: year 1 loss = $5,250, year 2 = $4,463, year 3 = $3,793. Total 3-year depreciation = $13,506. Add fuel, insurance, maintenance — total ownership cost of $0.50-0.80 per mile for a new midsize car.
