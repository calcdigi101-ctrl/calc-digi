Parking Cost Calculator – Daily, Monthly and Annual Expense

Calculate your total parking costs for work commuting, shopping trips, and events. Compare daily parking rates vs monthly passes, find your annual parking expense, and see how parking costs add to your total commute budget.

Parking is one of the most underestimated commuting expenses. In major US cities, monthly parking averages $300-600, making it a larger expense than fuel for many commuters. Urban workers paying $25/day for parking spend $6,250/year just on parking — before accounting for fuel, maintenance, and depreciation.

Formula

Daily Cost = Hourly Rate × Hours | Monthly Cost = Daily Rate × Work Days | Annual = Monthly × 12

Free and Cheaper Parking Strategies

Reduce parking costs: negotiate employer parking subsidy or pre-tax parking benefit (up to $315/month pre-tax in 2024), park farther from destination and walk (often 50-80% cheaper), use parking apps (SpotHero, ParkWhiz, BestParking) for discounts on garage parking, arrive early for time-limited free parking, use park-and-ride systems where available, carpool to split parking costs, and compare monthly pass vs daily rates.

Employer Parking Benefits

The IRS allows pre-tax parking benefits up to $315/month (2024), meaning employees can pay for parking with pre-tax dollars — effectively a 22-37% discount depending on tax bracket. Example: $300/month parking in a 24% tax bracket: after pre-tax benefit costs only $228/month effective ($72 tax savings). Always check if your employer offers a Qualified Transportation Fringe Benefit.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do people spend on parking per year? +
Average US urban commuter spending on parking: $1,500-3,000/year (suburban areas with subsidized or free parking). Major city commuters: $3,000-8,000/year. NYC: $6,000-9,600/year for downtown garage parking. This often exceeds annual fuel costs, making it the largest single commuting expense in dense urban areas.
Is monthly parking cheaper than daily? +
Almost always, monthly passes offer 20-40% savings vs daily rates. At $30/day daily parking, 22 work days = $660. Monthly pass might cost $400 — saving $260/month ($3,120/year). The break-even is typically 13-15 working days per month. If you commute more than 13 days/month, the monthly pass usually wins.
What are the pre-tax parking benefits? +
The IRS Qualified Transportation Fringe Benefit allows employers to offer up to $315/month (2024) in tax-free parking. Both employer-provided and employee-paid (via salary reduction) parking counts. At $315/month and 24% tax bracket: employee saves $75.60/month = $907/year in taxes. Check with your HR department about enrollment.
How much is parking in New York City? +
Manhattan daily parking: $35-80 at garages near major attractions and business districts. Some midtown garages: $50-80/day. Metered street parking where available: $4-7/hour. Monthly parking: $500-900 in Midtown Manhattan. Less expensive options: outer boroughs ($150-300/month), park at a subway station and take the train in.
Should I drive or take public transit to work? +
Compare: (Daily parking + daily gas + daily vehicle wear) vs (Daily transit cost). In most US cities with decent transit: monthly transit pass ($80-130) is far cheaper than parking alone ($200-500+). Add driving costs: total driving often costs 2-4x more than transit. The tradeoff is convenience and door-to-door travel time.
How do I find cheaper parking? +
Apps: SpotHero, ParkWhiz, and BestParking aggregate and discount garages and lots — often 20-30% cheaper than drive-up rates. ParkMobile and PayByPhone for meter parking. Parkmaven for free parking spot maps. Arrive early — many garages have early bird specials (arrive before 9am, leave before 7pm) at 40-60% off regular daily rates.
