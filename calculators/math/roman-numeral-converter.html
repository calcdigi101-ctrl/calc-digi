Subtractive Notation Rule

To avoid repeating a symbol more than 3 times, Roman numerals use subtraction: a smaller numeral before a larger one means subtract. Common combinations: IV=4, IX=9, XL=40, XC=90, CD=400, CM=900.

How do Roman numerals work? +
Symbols are added when in decreasing order (VI = 5+1=6) and subtracted when a smaller symbol precedes a larger one (IV = 5-1=4). This subtractive notation avoids repeating a symbol more than 3 times.
Why do we still see Roman numerals today? +
They're commonly used for clock faces, movie sequel numbering, book chapters, Super Bowl numbering, and formal document numbering for a classic, formal aesthetic.
What is the largest Roman numeral value? +
Using standard notation, Roman numerals typically represent numbers up to 3,999 (MMMCMXCIX). Beyond that, special notation with overlines (vinculum) is needed to multiply values by 1,000.
Is there a Roman numeral for zero? +
No, the classical Roman numeral system has no symbol for zero. The concept of zero as a number wasn't part of Roman mathematics; it later arrived in Europe through Arabic numerals.
