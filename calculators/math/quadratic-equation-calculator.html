Quadratic Equation Solver

Solve any quadratic equation in the form ax² + bx + c = 0 instantly. Shows real or complex roots, the discriminant, and the vertex of the parabola.

The Quadratic Formula

x = (-b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a

The expression under the square root, b² − 4ac, is called the discriminant and determines how many real solutions exist:

  • Discriminant > 0: Two distinct real roots
  • Discriminant = 0: One repeated real root (the vertex touches the x-axis)
  • Discriminant < 0: Two complex conjugate roots (no real solutions)

Worked Example

Solve x² − 5x + 6 = 0: here a=1, b=−5, c=6. Discriminant = (−5)² − 4(1)(6) = 25 − 24 = 1.

x = (5 ± √1) / 2 = (5 ± 1) / 2, giving x₁ = 3 and x₂ = 2.

What is the quadratic formula used for? +
It solves any quadratic equation (degree 2 polynomial) for its roots — the x-values where the equation equals zero. Used in physics (projectile motion), engineering, and optimization problems.
What if a equals 0? +
If a=0, the equation is no longer quadratic — it becomes linear (bx+c=0), with a single solution x = -c/b, solvable without the quadratic formula.
What are complex roots? +
When the discriminant is negative, roots involve the imaginary unit i (where i²=-1). These are written as a ± bi and represent valid mathematical solutions, just not real-number x-intercepts.
What is the vertex of a parabola? +
The vertex is the highest or lowest point of the parabola, found at x = -b/2a. It represents the minimum value (if a>0) or maximum value (if a<0) of the quadratic function.
