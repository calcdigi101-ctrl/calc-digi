GCF Calculator (Greatest Common Factor)

Find the Greatest Common Factor (also called GCD — Greatest Common Divisor) of two or more numbers instantly using the efficient Euclidean algorithm. Essential for simplifying fractions and solving number theory problems.

What is the Greatest Common Factor?

The Greatest Common Factor (GCF) of two or more numbers is the largest positive integer that divides all the given numbers exactly, leaving no remainder. It is also commonly referred to as the Greatest Common Divisor (GCD) or Highest Common Factor (HCF).

For example, to find GCF(24, 36): the factors of 24 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24 and the factors of 36 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36. The largest number appearing in both lists is 12, so GCF(24, 36) = 12.

Euclidean Algorithm: GCF(a, b) = GCF(b, a mod b), repeat until remainder = 0

The Euclidean algorithm is far more efficient than listing all factors, especially for large numbers. It repeatedly applies division until the remainder reaches zero — the last nonzero remainder is the GCF.

Step-by-Step Example

Find GCF(48, 18) using the Euclidean algorithm:

  1. 48 ÷ 18 = 2 remainder 12
  2. 18 ÷ 12 = 1 remainder 6
  3. 12 ÷ 6 = 2 remainder 0
  4. Remainder is 0, so GCF(48, 18) = 6

Real-World Uses of GCF

  • Simplifying fractions: Divide numerator and denominator by GCF to get lowest terms
  • Splitting into equal groups: GCF tells you the maximum group size for equal distribution
  • Cutting material: Finding the largest equal piece you can cut from multiple lengths
  • Algebra: Factoring out the GCF from polynomial expressions

What is the GCF of 12 and 18? +
The GCF is 6. Both 12 and 18 divide evenly by 6 (12÷6=2, 18÷6=3), and no larger number works for both.
How is GCF used in simplifying fractions? +
Divide both numerator and denominator by their GCF to fully simplify a fraction. For 12/18, GCF=6, so 12/18 simplifies to 2/3.
What is the difference between GCF and LCM? +
GCF is the largest number that divides all given numbers. LCM is the smallest number that all given numbers divide into. They are related by: GCF(a,b) × LCM(a,b) = a × b.
Can GCF be used for more than two numbers? +
Yes. For three or more numbers, compute GCF iteratively: GCF(a,b,c) = GCF(GCF(a,b), c). Our calculator handles any number of inputs automatically.
