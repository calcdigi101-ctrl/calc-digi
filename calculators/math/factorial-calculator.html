What is a Factorial?

The factorial of a non-negative integer n, written as n!, is the product of all positive integers from 1 to n.

n! = n × (n−1) × (n−2) × ... × 2 × 1

Factorials grow extremely rapidly: 10! = 3,628,800 while 20! has 19 digits and 100! has 158 digits.

What is 0 factorial? +
0! = 1 by definition. This keeps combinatorics formulas consistent — "choosing 0 items from n" should equal exactly 1 way (doing nothing).
Where are factorials used? +
Permutations (nPr = n!/(n-r)!), combinations (nCr = n!/(r!(n-r)!)), probability calculations, Taylor series in calculus, and many statistical distributions like Poisson and binomial.
Why does factorial grow so fast? +
Each new factorial multiplies by one more integer. 10! = 3.6 million, but 20! ≈ 2.4 quintillion. This super-exponential growth makes factorials impractical to calculate by hand for large n.
Can factorial be calculated for negative numbers? +
Not using the simple product definition. However, the Gamma function extends factorial to non-integers and negative non-integers: Γ(n) = (n-1)! for positive integers.
