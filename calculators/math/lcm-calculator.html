LCM Calculator (Least Common Multiple)

Find the Least Common Multiple of two or more numbers instantly using the GCF method. Essential for adding fractions with different denominators and solving scheduling problems.

What is the Least Common Multiple?

The Least Common Multiple (LCM) is the smallest positive integer that is divisible by all of the given numbers without a remainder. It is widely used in fraction arithmetic, scheduling, and number theory.

LCM(a, b) = (a × b) / GCF(a, b)

For more than two numbers, apply iteratively: LCM(a,b,c) = LCM(LCM(a,b), c).

Step-by-Step Example

Find LCM(12, 18): First find GCF(12,18) = 6. Then LCM = (12 × 18) / 6 = 216 / 6 = 36. Verify: 36 ÷ 12 = 3 ✓, 36 ÷ 18 = 2 ✓.

Real-World Uses of LCM

  • Adding fractions: Find a common denominator for fractions like 1/4 + 1/6 using LCM(4,6)=12
  • Scheduling: Two events repeating every 4 and 6 days will coincide every LCM(4,6)=12 days
  • Gear ratios: Engineers use LCM to find when gear teeth realign
  • Music: Finding when rhythmic patterns with different lengths will sync up

What is LCM(4,6)? +
LCM(4,6) = 12. It is the smallest number both 4 and 6 divide into evenly (4×3=12, 6×2=12).
How is LCM used in adding fractions? +
To add 1/4 + 1/6, find LCM(4,6)=12. Convert: 3/12 + 2/12 = 5/12. The LCM becomes the common denominator.
Is LCM always larger than the given numbers? +
Yes, LCM is always ≥ the largest of the given numbers. If one number is a multiple of the other, the LCM equals the larger number (e.g., LCM(4,8)=8).
Can LCM be calculated for more than 2 numbers? +
Yes. Compute iteratively: LCM(a,b,c) = LCM(LCM(a,b),c). Our calculator handles any number of inputs automatically.
