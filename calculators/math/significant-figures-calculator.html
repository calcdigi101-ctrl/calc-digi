Significant Figures Calculator

Round any number to a specified number of significant figures and count how many sig figs a number contains. Essential for science, engineering, and chemistry coursework.

Significant Figure Rules

  • Non-zero digits are always significant (1–9)
  • Zeros between non-zero digits are significant (e.g., 1004 has 4 sig figs)
  • Leading zeros are NOT significant (0.0034 has 2 sig figs)
  • Trailing zeros after decimal point ARE significant (3.40 has 3 sig figs)
  • Trailing zeros in whole numbers are ambiguous without a decimal point

How do you count significant figures? +
Count all non-zero digits, zeros between non-zeros, and trailing zeros after a decimal. Ignore leading zeros. So 0.00340 has 3 sig figs: 3, 4, and the trailing zero.
Why do significant figures matter in science? +
They communicate measurement precision. If a ruler measures to 1mm, recording 2.3450 cm implies false precision — 2.35 cm is appropriate. Sig figs ensure results match the actual accuracy of measurements.
How do you round to 3 significant figures? +
Find the 3rd significant digit, look at the next digit, and apply standard rounding. For 0.004567: the sig figs are 4, 5, 6. The next digit is 7 ≥ 5, so round up → 0.00457.
How many sig figs does 100 have? +
Ambiguous without a decimal point — could be 1, 2, or 3 sig figs. Writing 100. (with decimal) signals 3 sig figs. Using scientific notation (1.00 × 10²) removes the ambiguity entirely.
