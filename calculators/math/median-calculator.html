How to Find the Median

Odd count: median = middle value of sorted data
Even count: median = (middle₁ + middle₂) / 2

For {3, 1, 4, 1, 5, 9}: sorted → {1, 1, 3, 4, 5, 9}. Even count (6), so median = (3+4)/2 = 3.5.

Mean vs Median — When to Use Each

  • Use mean: Symmetric distributions, no extreme outliers, when total matters
  • Use median: Skewed data, income/wealth data, housing prices, any dataset with outliers
  • Example: A group of 9 people earning $30k–$50k and 1 earning $10M — mean ≈ $1M (misleading), median ≈ $38k (representative)

What is the median of an even number of values? +
Sort the values, then average the two middle ones. For {2, 4, 6, 8}: middle values are 4 and 6, so median = (4+6)/2 = 5.
Why is median better than mean for income data? +
Income is right-skewed — a small number of very high earners can dramatically inflate the mean. The median is unaffected by extreme outliers, giving a more representative "typical" income.
What is the median of a single number? +
The median of a single-element dataset is that number itself, since there is only one value and it is both the smallest and largest.
How does the median relate to quartiles? +
The median is the second quartile (Q2) — it splits the sorted data into two equal halves. Q1 splits the lower half; Q3 splits the upper half. Together they define the interquartile range (IQR).
