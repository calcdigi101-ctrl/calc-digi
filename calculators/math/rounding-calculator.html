Rounding Calculator

Round any number to any decimal place — from nearest 1000 down to 5 decimal places. Uses the standard half-up rounding rule and shows all common roundings at once.

Rounding Rules

Standard (half-up): digit ≥ 5 → round up; digit < 5 → round down

To round 3.14159 to 2 decimal places: look at the 3rd decimal (1). Since 1 < 5, keep 2nd decimal unchanged → 3.14.

To round 3.145 to 2 decimal places: look at the 3rd decimal (5). Since 5 ≥ 5, round up → 3.15.

How do you round to 2 decimal places? +
Look at the third decimal place. If it is 5 or more, add 1 to the second decimal place and drop everything after. If less than 5, simply drop everything after the second decimal place.
What is the difference between rounding and truncating? +
Rounding considers the next digit (3.75 → 3.8). Truncating simply cuts off digits (3.75 → 3.7). Truncating always rounds toward zero, while rounding can go either way.
How do you round to the nearest 100? +
Look at the tens digit. For 347: tens digit is 4 (less than 5), so round down → 300. For 371: tens digit is 7 (≥5), round up → 400.
What is banker's rounding? +
Banker's rounding (round half to even) rounds 0.5 to the nearest even number: 2.5 → 2, 3.5 → 4. This reduces cumulative rounding bias in financial calculations.
