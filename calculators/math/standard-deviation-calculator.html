Standard Deviation Calculator

Calculate population or sample standard deviation, variance, and mean instantly from any set of numbers. Essential for statistics homework and data analysis.

Standard Deviation Formula

Population SD: σ = √(Σ(x − μ)² / N)
Sample SD: s = √(Σ(x − x̄)² / (N−1))

Use population SD when your data represents the entire group of interest. Use sample SD when your data is a subset used to estimate a larger population's variability — this is the more common case in research.

Step-by-Step Calculation

  1. Calculate the mean (average) of all values
  2. Subtract the mean from each value, then square the result
  3. Sum all squared differences
  4. Divide by N (population) or N-1 (sample) to get variance
  5. Take the square root of variance to get standard deviation

When do I use sample vs population standard deviation? +
Use population SD (divide by N) when you have data for the entire population. Use sample SD (divide by N-1) when your data is a sample used to estimate population variability — this is more common in research and most statistics courses.
What is a "good" standard deviation? +
There's no universal "good" value — it depends entirely on context and what's being measured. Compare standard deviation relative to the mean (coefficient of variation) for meaningful interpretation across different datasets.
What does it mean if standard deviation is 0? +
A standard deviation of 0 means all values in the dataset are identical — there is no variation at all from the mean.
How many data points do I need for standard deviation? +
Mathematically, you need at least 2 data points. However, for sample standard deviation to be a meaningful estimate of population variability, more data points (typically 30+) provide more reliable results.
