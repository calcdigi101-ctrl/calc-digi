Snowball vs Avalanche Method

Debt Snowball: Pay minimums on all debts, then put extra money toward the smallest balance first. Once paid off, roll that payment into the next smallest debt. Best for psychological motivation.

Debt Avalanche: Pay minimums on all debts, then put extra money toward the highest interest rate debt first. This minimizes total interest paid mathematically.

Monthly Interest = (Balance × Annual Rate) ÷ 12

Research from Northwestern's Kellogg School found that the snowball method actually leads to higher overall debt payoff success rates due to the motivational "quick win" effect — even though avalanche saves more money mathematically.

Should I use snowball or avalanche? +
Avalanche saves more money. Snowball provides faster motivation through quick wins. If you've struggled to stick with debt payoff plans before, snowball's psychological boost may serve you better despite slightly higher total interest.
Should I pay off debt or invest first? +
Generally, pay off debt with interest rates above 6-7% before investing, since guaranteed "return" from debt payoff often exceeds expected investment returns. Always capture any employer 401k match first, regardless of debt.
How does extra payment affect payoff time? +
Even small extra payments dramatically reduce payoff time and interest due to compound interest working in reverse. An extra $50/month on a credit card can cut years off the payoff timeline.
Should I consolidate my debts? +
Debt consolidation can help if you secure a lower interest rate and avoid taking on new debt. Be cautious of consolidation loans with fees that offset interest savings, and avoid extending the loan term excessively.
