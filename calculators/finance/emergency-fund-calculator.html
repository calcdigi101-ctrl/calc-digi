Calculate exactly how much you need in your emergency fund based on essential monthly expenses and your personal risk factors — job stability, dependents, and income sources.

How Much Emergency Fund Do You Need?

Emergency Fund = Essential Monthly Expenses × Number of Months

The standard recommendation is 3-6 months of essential expenses, but your personal number should reflect your specific risk factors:

  • 3 months: Dual income household, stable jobs, no dependents, good health insurance
  • 6 months: Single income, some job instability, or one dependent
  • 9-12 months: Self-employed, commission-based income, unstable industry, multiple dependents

What to Include in Essential Expenses

✅ Include
  • Rent/mortgage
  • Utilities
  • Groceries
  • Insurance premiums
  • Minimum debt payments
  • Transportation
❌ Exclude
  • Dining out
  • Entertainment
  • Subscriptions
  • Vacations
  • Shopping
  • Gym memberships

How much should I have saved for emergencies? +
3-6 months of essential expenses is standard advice. Adjust based on job stability, income sources, and dependents. Self-employed individuals should target 9-12 months due to less predictable income.
Should I pay off debt before building an emergency fund? +
Build a small starter emergency fund ($1,000-$2,000) first, then focus on high-interest debt, then build your full emergency fund. This prevents new debt from emergencies while you tackle existing debt.
Where should I keep my emergency fund? +
A high-yield savings account is ideal — liquid, FDIC-insured, and earning some interest (typically 4-5% currently). Avoid CDs (penalty for early withdrawal) or investments (market risk) for true emergency funds.
What counts as a true emergency? +
Job loss, medical emergencies, essential home/car repairs, and unexpected necessary travel (family emergency). Planned expenses like vacations or predictable annual costs (insurance premiums) should have separate savings funds.
