APR Calculator

Calculate the true Annual Percentage Rate (APR) on a loan by factoring in upfront fees. See how fees impact your real borrowing cost beyond the advertised interest rate.

APR vs Interest Rate — What's the Difference?

The interest rate is simply the cost of borrowing the principal amount. APR goes further by including upfront fees (origination fees, points, closing costs) spread over the loan term, expressed as a yearly percentage.

APR accounts for: Interest Rate + Fees ÷ Loan Term

This is why lenders are legally required to disclose APR — it gives borrowers a true apples-to-apples comparison between loan offers, even when fee structures differ significantly.

Common Loan Fees That Affect APR

  • Origination fee: 0.5-1% of loan amount, charged by lender for processing
  • Discount points: Prepaid interest to lower your rate, 1 point = 1% of loan amount
  • Closing costs (mortgages): Appraisal, title insurance, attorney fees — typically 2-5% of loan
  • Application fees: Fixed fee for processing the loan application
  • Mortgage insurance: PMI or MIP, required for low down payment loans

Why does my loan have a higher APR than interest rate? +
This happens because of upfront fees. A $200,000 mortgage at 6% interest with $4,000 in fees might show an APR of 6.2-6.3%, reflecting those fees spread over the loan term as an annualized cost.
Should I always choose the lowest APR loan? +
Generally yes for like-for-like comparisons, but consider your timeline. If you plan to pay off or refinance quickly, a loan with higher fees but lower rate (lower APR) may not be optimal since you won't benefit from the lower rate long enough to offset upfront costs.
Does APR apply to credit cards too? +
Yes, but credit card APR typically does not include annual fees in the same way as loan APR. Credit card APR usually just reflects the interest rate charged on carried balances, compounded based on your billing cycle.
Is a 0% APR offer ever a bad deal? +
0% APR promotional offers can still involve fees or deferred interest that kicks in retroactively if not paid off in time. Always read the fine print — "0% APR for 12 months" sometimes means full back-interest is charged if not paid by month 12.
