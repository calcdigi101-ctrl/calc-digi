How Net Worth is Calculated

Net worth is the simplest and most comprehensive measure of your overall financial health. It represents what you truly own after accounting for everything you owe.

Net Worth = Total Assets − Total Liabilities

Assets include: Cash and savings, checking accounts, investment accounts (401k, IRA, brokerage), home value, vehicle value, and other valuable property.

Liabilities include: Mortgage balance, auto loans, credit card debt, student loans, personal loans, and any other money owed.

What counts as an asset for net worth? +
Cash, savings, checking accounts, investments (stocks, 401k, IRA), home value, vehicle value, and other valuable property like jewelry or collectibles. Use current market value, not purchase price.
Is a negative net worth bad? +
Negative net worth is common for young adults with student loans and is not necessarily alarming if you have stable income and a plan to pay down debt. The trend over time matters more than a single snapshot.
How often should I calculate my net worth? +
Quarterly or annually is sufficient for most people. Tracking too frequently can cause unnecessary stress from market fluctuations in investment accounts. Focus on the long-term trend rather than short-term changes.
Should I count my car as an asset? +
Yes, but use realistic current resale value (not purchase price), as cars depreciate quickly. Some financial planners exclude vehicles since they are depreciating assets you need for daily life rather than wealth-building assets.
