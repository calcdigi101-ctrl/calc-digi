How 401k Growth is Calculated

Your 401k balance grows through three combined forces: your contributions, your employer's matching contributions, and compound investment returns on the total balance over time.

Total Contribution = (Salary × Your % + Salary × Match %) per year

Most employers match 50-100% of your contribution up to a certain percentage of salary (commonly 3-6%). Not contributing enough to get the full match means leaving free money on the table.

How much should I contribute to my 401k? +
At minimum, contribute enough to get the full employer match. Beyond that, financial experts recommend saving 15% of income for retirement total (across all accounts). Increase contributions with every raise.
What happens to my 401k if I leave my job? +
You can leave it with your former employer (if allowed), roll it into your new employer's 401k, or roll it into an IRA. Avoid cashing out, as this triggers taxes and a 10% early withdrawal penalty if under 59½.
Should I choose Traditional or Roth 401k? +
Traditional 401k gives a tax deduction now, with taxes paid in retirement. Roth 401k is taxed now but grows tax-free. Choose Roth if you expect to be in a higher tax bracket in retirement; choose Traditional if you expect a lower bracket.
What is a realistic 401k growth rate to assume? +
Most financial planners use 6-8% annual return assumptions for diversified stock/bond portfolios over long time horizons (20+ years). This is more conservative than historical S&P 500 average to account for fees and market variability.
