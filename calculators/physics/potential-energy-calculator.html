Potential Energy – Gravitational and Elastic Forms

Potential energy (PE) is energy stored in an object due to its position, arrangement, or condition — ready to be converted to kinetic energy or do work. The two most common forms in classical physics are gravitational potential energy (due to height above a reference) and elastic potential energy (due to deformation of a spring or elastic material).

The law of conservation of energy states that in a closed system, total mechanical energy (KE + PE) remains constant. A ball dropped from height h: at the top, all energy is PE = mgh. As it falls, PE converts to KE. Just before hitting the ground: all energy is KE = ½mv² = mgh, giving v = √(2gh). This relationship between height and impact velocity is used in everything from roller coaster design to ballistics.

Conservation of Energy – Practical Examples

Roller coaster: starting at height h = 40m above ground (bottom of first drop). PE = mgh = 70kg × 9.81 × 40 = 27,468 J. At bottom: all converted to KE. v = √(2gh) = √(2 × 9.81 × 40) = 28.0 m/s = 100.7 km/h. Real coasters achieve ~90-95% of this (friction/air resistance losses). Pendulum: at max height h, all PE. At bottom, all KE. Period T = 2π√(L/g) — independent of mass and amplitude for small swings. Hydroelectric dam: water at height h = 100m. PE per kg = mgh = 1×9.81×100 = 981 J/kg. Flow rate 500 m³/s = 500,000 kg/s. Power = 500,000 × 981 = 490.5 MW (theoretical, practical ~85% = 417 MW).

Elastic Potential Energy – Springs and Hooke's Law

Hooke's Law: F = -kx (spring force = spring constant × displacement, restoring direction). Elastic PE = ½kx². Spring constant k: rubber band ~100 N/m, car suspension spring ~20,000 N/m, stiff industrial spring ~100,000 N/m. A car suspension spring (k = 25,000 N/m) compressed 0.1m: PE = ½ × 25,000 × 0.1² = 125 J. This energy is released to restore the car body as the wheel rebounds. Simple harmonic motion: mass on spring oscillates with period T = 2π√(m/k). Energy alternates between KE (at equilibrium) and PE (at maximum displacement) — total conserved.

Chemical and Nuclear Potential Energy

Potential energy extends beyond mechanical systems. Chemical PE: stored in molecular bonds. Combustion releases chemical PE as heat and light. Gasoline: ~46 MJ/kg energy density. TNT: ~4.6 MJ/kg. Batteries: lithium-ion ~0.7 MJ/kg. Nuclear PE: stored in atomic nuclei. Fission of uranium releases ~80 million times more energy per kg than chemical explosion. Nuclear PE arises from the strong nuclear force binding protons and neutrons. Mass-energy equivalence: E = mc². Even 1g of mass contains c² × 0.001 = 9×10¹³ J — equivalent to Hiroshima bomb energy.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is gravitational potential energy? +
PE = mgh. Mass (kg) × gravity (9.81 m/s²) × height (m) = Joules. 70 kg person on 3rd floor (10m high): PE = 70 × 9.81 × 10 = 6,867 J relative to ground.
What is the relationship between PE and KE? +
Conservation of energy: total mechanical energy = KE + PE = constant (no friction). Object falling from height h: PE = mgh at top → KE = ½mv² at bottom. Impact velocity: v = √(2gh).
What is elastic potential energy? +
PE_spring = ½kx². k = spring constant (N/m), x = compression/stretch (m). Spring k=1000 N/m compressed 0.05m: PE = ½ × 1000 × 0.0025 = 1.25 J.
How does a hydroelectric dam use potential energy? +
Water stored at height h has PE = mgh per unit mass. Falling through turbines converts PE → KE → electrical energy. 100m dam, 500 kg/s flow: Power = 500 × 9.81 × 100 = 490,500 W ≈ 490 kW (theoretical).
What is reference point for PE? +
PE is always relative to a chosen reference point (PE = 0). Only changes in PE matter physically. Choose convenient reference: ground level, lowest point of motion, sea level. ΔPE = mgΔh.
What units is potential energy measured in? +
Joules (J). 1 J = 1 kg·m²/s² = 1 N·m. Large-scale: kJ, MJ, GJ. Food energy: Calories (1 Cal = 4,184 J). Electricity: kWh (1 kWh = 3,600,000 J).
