Momentum Calculator – p = mv and Conservation of Momentum

Calculate linear momentum (p = mv), solve conservation of momentum for elastic and inelastic collisions, and find impulse (J = FΔt). Momentum is conserved in all isolated systems — a fundamental principle of physics.

Momentum (p = mv) is the product of mass and velocity — a measure of how difficult it is to stop a moving object. A heavy truck at slow speed and a light bullet at high speed can have the same momentum. In any isolated system, total momentum before a collision equals total momentum after, regardless of collision type.

Momentum and Collisions – Complete Physics Guide

Linear momentum (p) is defined as the product of an object's mass and its velocity: p = mv. It is a vector quantity — it has the same direction as velocity. The SI unit of momentum is kg·m/s (equivalent to N·s). Momentum quantifies the "quantity of motion" — a 2000 kg truck at 10 m/s has the same momentum (20,000 kg·m/s) as a 0.02 kg bullet traveling at 1,000,000 m/s (if such speeds were possible).

The Law of Conservation of Momentum states that in an isolated system (no external forces), the total momentum remains constant. This is one of the most fundamental conservation laws in physics, applying from subatomic particle collisions to galaxy cluster interactions. It derives directly from Newton's Third Law: action and reaction forces in a collision are equal and opposite, so momentum transferred between objects cancels exactly.

Impulse-Momentum Theorem

Impulse (J) = Force × time = F × Δt. Impulse equals the change in momentum: J = Δp = m(v_f - v_i). This is why safety features extend collision time — longer time for same momentum change = smaller force. Car crumple zones: extend impact duration from 0.05s to 0.15s, reducing peak force by 3×. Airbags: cushion impact over 0.05-0.15s vs 0.002s for bare impact. Sports: follow-through in baseball/tennis maximizes impulse (force × time contact). Catching: pulling hands back with ball extends time, reduces impact force (hand velocity matches ball before stopping).

Conservation of Momentum in Collisions

Elastic collision (two objects, 1D): Object 1 (m₁, v₁) hits stationary Object 2 (m₂, v₂=0). After: v₁' = v₁(m₁-m₂)/(m₁+m₂). v₂' = 2m₁v₁/(m₁+m₂). Equal masses (m₁=m₂): v₁'=0, v₂'=v₁. Object 1 stops completely — all momentum transferred to Object 2. This is why billiard cue ball stops after hitting another ball head-on. Perfectly inelastic: both stick together. v_final = (m₁v₁+m₂v₂)/(m₁+m₂). A 1500kg car at 20 m/s hits stationary 1500kg car: v_final = (1500×20)/(1500+1500) = 10 m/s. KE lost: ½(3000)(10²) - ½(1500)(20²) = 150,000 - 300,000 = -150,000 J lost to deformation.

Rocket Propulsion – Conservation of Momentum

Rockets work by conservation of momentum. Exhaust gases are ejected backward with high momentum — equal and opposite momentum is imparted to the rocket (forward). The Tsiolkovsky rocket equation: Δv = v_e × ln(m₀/m_f), where v_e is exhaust velocity, m₀ is initial mass (full fuel), m_f is final mass (empty). To reach orbit (~7,800 m/s), with v_e = 3,000 m/s: m₀/m_f = e^(7800/3000) = e^2.6 = 13.5. Must be 93% fuel by mass. This is why rockets are mostly fuel and why staged rockets are used — discarding empty tanks reduces m₀ for subsequent stages.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is momentum? +
p = mv. Mass × velocity. 1000 kg car at 20 m/s: p = 20,000 kg·m/s. 0.01 kg bullet at 900 m/s: p = 9 kg·m/s. Car has 2222× more momentum despite bullet's high speed.
What is conservation of momentum? +
Total momentum of isolated system is constant. m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = m₁v₁' + m₂v₂' (before = after). Applies to all collisions. Newton's 3rd Law guarantees it — internal forces cancel.
What is impulse? +
J = F × Δt = change in momentum. Larger force OR longer contact time = more impulse. Car crumple zones extend time → reduce peak force. J = m(v_f - v_i).
Elastic vs inelastic collision? +
Elastic: kinetic energy conserved, objects separate (billiard balls). Inelastic: KE not conserved (lost to heat/sound/deformation). Perfectly inelastic: objects stick together — maximum KE loss.
How does rocket propulsion use momentum? +
Rocket ejects exhaust backward (high momentum). Equal forward momentum imparted to rocket (Newton's 3rd Law). No external forces needed — works in vacuum. Total system momentum = 0 before launch.
What is the unit of momentum? +
kg·m/s = N·s (Newton-second). Impulse-momentum theorem: impulse (N·s) = momentum change (kg·m/s). Both have same units.
