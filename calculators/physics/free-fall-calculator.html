Free Fall Physics – Gravitational Acceleration Guide

Free fall describes the motion of an object falling solely under the influence of gravity, with no air resistance. Galileo Galilei (1589-1592) demonstrated the revolutionary principle that all objects fall at the same rate regardless of their mass — a 1 kg ball and a 100 kg ball dropped simultaneously hit the ground at the same time (in vacuum). This contradicted Aristotle's 2000-year-old belief that heavier objects fall faster.

Standard gravitational acceleration at Earth's surface: g = 9.80665 m/s² (defined standard). Commonly used: g = 9.81 m/s². The value varies slightly: equator (9.78 m/s²) vs poles (9.83 m/s²), due to Earth's rotation and equatorial bulge. At 100km altitude (edge of space): g ≈ 9.52 m/s² — gravity barely diminishes at this scale.

Free Fall on Other Planets

Gravitational acceleration varies by planet. Impact velocity from 10m height on different worlds: Earth (g=9.81): v = √(2×9.81×10) = 14.0 m/s. Moon (g=1.62): v = √(2×1.62×10) = 5.7 m/s. Mars (g=3.72): v = √(2×3.72×10) = 8.6 m/s. Jupiter surface (g=24.79): v = √(2×24.79×10) = 22.3 m/s. This is why lunar rovers fall slowly and why landing on Mars requires special deceleration techniques (parachutes work poorly in thin atmosphere — hence the Curiosity rover's sky crane system).

Galileo's Experiment and Einstein's Equivalence Principle

Galileo's discovery that all objects fall at the same rate (equivalence of gravitational and inertial mass) was later elevated by Einstein to a fundamental principle: the Equivalence Principle — the basis of General Relativity. In an accelerating elevator or rocket (no windows), you cannot tell the difference between acceleration and gravity by any local experiment. This thought experiment led Einstein to realize gravity is not a force but a curvature of spacetime. The Apollo 15 astronaut David Scott demonstrated this on the Moon in 1971, simultaneously dropping a hammer and feather on live TV — both hit the surface at the same time, confirming Galileo in vacuum.

Free Fall in Engineering – Falling Object Safety

Construction safety calculations use free fall physics. A tool dropped from scaffold height h: impact velocity v = √(2gh). From 10m: v = 14.0 m/s = 50.4 km/h. From 50m: v = 31.3 m/s = 112.7 km/h. Kinetic energy at impact: KE = ½mv² = mgh. A 0.5 kg wrench from 10m: KE = 0.5 × 9.81 × 10 = 49.1 J — equivalent to being hit by a baseball bat. This is why hard hats and tool tethers are mandatory on construction sites. Elevator safety: counterweight and governor brakes stop freefall. Emergency brakes engage when cable snaps — brake springs compress against guide rails, decelerating at 3-5g.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is free fall? +
Motion under gravity alone (no air resistance). All objects accelerate at g = 9.81 m/s² regardless of mass. After t seconds: v = 9.81t m/s, d = 4.905t² m.
How fast does an object fall from 100m? +
v = √(2gh) = √(2 × 9.81 × 100) = √1962 = 44.3 m/s = 159.5 km/h. Time: t = √(2h/g) = √(200/9.81) = 4.52 seconds.
Do heavy objects fall faster? +
No — in vacuum, all objects fall at identical acceleration g = 9.81 m/s². Galileo proved this. With air resistance, more aerodynamic objects fall faster. A feather and hammer fall together on the Moon (no atmosphere).
What is terminal velocity? +
When air drag equals gravity, acceleration = 0 and velocity is constant. Skydiver spread position: ~54 m/s (195 km/h). Diving headfirst: ~90 m/s. Raindrop: 9 m/s. Terminal velocity = √(2mg/ρCdA).
How to calculate time to fall a given distance? +
t = √(2h/g). Fall 50m from rest: t = √(2×50/9.81) = √10.19 = 3.19 seconds. With initial downward velocity v₀: solve ½gt² + v₀t - h = 0 (quadratic formula).
What is g on the Moon and Mars? +
Moon: g = 1.62 m/s² (1/6 Earth). Mars: g = 3.72 m/s² (0.38 Earth). Jupiter surface: g = 24.79 m/s² (2.53 Earth). Objects fall much slower on Moon — a 1m drop takes 1.11s vs 0.45s on Earth.
