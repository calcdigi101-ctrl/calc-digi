How Time Zones Work

The world is divided into 24 time zones, each roughly 15° of longitude wide. All zones are expressed as an offset from UTC (Coordinated Universal Time) — the global time standard.

Target Time = Source Time + (Target UTC offset − Source UTC offset)

For example: London (UTC+0) to Dubai (UTC+4) — if it's 10:00 AM in London, add 4 hours → 2:00 PM in Dubai.

What is UTC and how does it work? +
UTC is the primary time standard by which the world regulates clocks. It doesn't observe daylight saving time, making it the ideal reference point. All time zones are expressed as UTC ± offset hours.
Does daylight saving time affect conversions? +
Yes, and our converter handles this automatically using the browser's Intl API. During DST periods, affected zones (US, EU, Australia) shift by +1 hour, which changes the offset difference between zones.
What is IST to EST difference? +
IST is UTC+5:30 and EST is UTC-5, a difference of 10.5 hours. So 12:00 PM IST = 1:30 AM EST. During US daylight saving (EDT, UTC-4), the gap is 9.5 hours.
What is PKT (Pakistan Standard Time)? +
PKT is UTC+5 and is used throughout Pakistan. It does not observe daylight saving time, so the offset is constant year-round. Pakistan is 5 hours ahead of UTC.
