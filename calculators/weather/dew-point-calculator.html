Dew Point Calculator – Temperature, Humidity and Comfort

Calculate the dew point temperature from air temperature and relative humidity. Understand what your dew point means for comfort levels, fog formation, frost risk, and why meteorologists prefer dew point over relative humidity for measuring atmospheric moisture.

The dew point is the temperature at which air becomes saturated and moisture condenses into dew, fog, or frost. Unlike relative humidity (which changes with temperature), dew point directly measures the actual amount of moisture in the air — making it a more reliable indicator of comfort and condensation risk.

Formula

Dew Point (°C) ≈ T - ((100 - RH) / 5) | Magnus formula: Td = (243.04 × [ln(RH/100) + 17.625T/(243.04+T)]) / (17.625 - [ln(RH/100) + 17.625T/(243.04+T)])

Dew Point Comfort Scale

Meteorologists use dew point rather than relative humidity to describe how humid the air feels, because dew point remains constant as temperature changes while relative humidity changes throughout the day. A dew point of 65°F (18°C) feels humid whether the temperature is 75°F or 95°F.

Dew Point vs Relative Humidity

Relative humidity (RH) expresses moisture as a percentage of the maximum moisture air can hold at that temperature. As temperature rises during the day, the same amount of moisture becomes a smaller percentage of the capacity — RH drops even though no moisture left the air. Dew point stays constant throughout the day unless actual moisture changes, making it superior for comfort assessment.

Fog and Frost Formation

Fog forms when air temperature drops to the dew point — the air becomes saturated and water droplets suspend in the air. Radiation fog forms on clear calm nights when ground radiates heat: surface air cools to its dew point. Frost forms when the dew point is below freezing (32°F/0°C) — water vapor deposits directly as ice crystals. Frost advisories are issued when temperatures are expected to drop to the frost point.

Indoor Humidity and Dew Point

Indoor dew point affects comfort and structural health. Below 40°F (4°C) dew point indoors: very dry, static electricity, irritated sinuses. 40-50°F (4-10°C): comfortable, typical winter indoor air. Above 60°F (16°C): feels humid, risk of mold growth. Ideal indoor dew point: 40-55°F (4-13°C). In winter, running a humidifier raises indoor dew point; in summer, air conditioning lowers it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is dew point? +
Dew point is the temperature to which air must be cooled (at constant pressure and water vapor content) for saturation to occur. When air temperature reaches the dew point, condensation forms as dew, fog, or frost. Higher dew point = more moisture in the air = more humid and uncomfortable conditions.
What is a comfortable dew point? +
Most people find dew points below 60°F (16°C) comfortable. 55-60°F feels slightly humid but pleasant. Above 65°F (18°C) starts to feel oppressive. Above 70°F (21°C) is very uncomfortable for most people. The highest dew points in the US occur in the Gulf Coast states and Midwest during summer, regularly reaching 70-75°F.
How do you calculate dew point from temperature and humidity? +
Simple approximation: Dew Point ≈ Temperature - ((100 - Relative Humidity) / 5). For more accuracy, use the Magnus formula: Td = 243.04 × [ln(RH/100) + 17.625T/(243.04+T)] / [17.625 - ln(RH/100) - 17.625T/(243.04+T)]. This calculator uses the accurate Magnus formula.
What dew point causes frost? +
Frost forms when the dew point (or more precisely, the frost point) is at or below 32°F (0°C) and surface temperatures drop to that level. Light frost: 28-32°F. Moderate frost: 24-28°F. Severe frost: below 24°F. Gardeners monitor dew point to protect sensitive plants during spring and fall when overnight frost is possible.
Why do meteorologists prefer dew point over relative humidity? +
Relative humidity changes dramatically with temperature — a 50% RH at 60°F becomes 25% RH at 80°F with the same actual moisture content. Dew point stays constant as long as the actual water vapor content does not change, making it a reliable indicator of how humid it actually is. A dew point of 65°F always feels humid regardless of air temperature.
What is the highest dew point ever recorded? +
The highest recorded dew point was approximately 95°F (35°C) in Dhahran, Saudi Arabia on July 8, 2003 — making the heat index approximately 176°F (80°C). In the United States, dew points above 80°F (27°C) are extremely rare. Regular Gulf Coast summer dew points of 75°F are already considered very oppressive.
