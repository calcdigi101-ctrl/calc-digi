pH Calculator – Hydrogen Ion Concentration and Acid-Base pH

Calculate pH from [H⁺], pOH, or Ka. Convert between pH, pOH, [H⁺], and [OH⁻]. Essential for chemistry, biology, medicine, and environmental science acid-base calculations.

pH measures hydrogen ion concentration on a log scale from 0-14. pH 7 = neutral, below 7 = acidic, above 7 = basic. Each unit = 10-fold change in [H⁺]. Blood must stay pH 7.35-7.45 for survival.

Formula

pH = -log[H⁺] | [H⁺] = 10^(-pH) | pH + pOH = 14 | Weak acid: pH = ½(pKa - log C)

Why pH Matters

Blood pH 7.35-7.45 is critical — pH 7.0 causes fatal arrhythmias. Pool water needs pH 7.2-7.8 for effective chlorination. Most enzymes function at pH 7.4. Soil pH 6.0-7.0 ideal for plants.

Logarithmic Scale

Each pH unit = 10× change in [H⁺]. pH 3 is 100× more acidic than pH 5. At pH 7: [H⁺] = 10⁻⁷ mol/L.

Ka and Weak Acids

Ka = acid dissociation constant. pKa = -log(Ka). For weak acid concentration C: pH ≈ ½(pKa - log C). Acetic acid: Ka = 1.8×10⁻⁵, pKa = 4.75.

pH in Chemistry and Biology

pH is one of the most important parameters in chemistry and biology. It affects reaction rates, protein structure, enzyme activity, drug ionization, and almost every biological process. Understanding pH calculation and its implications is fundamental to chemistry education and laboratory practice.

Strong vs Weak Acids

Strong acids (HCl, H₂SO₄, HNO₃) dissociate completely: [H⁺] = initial concentration. 0.01 M HCl: [H⁺] = 0.01, pH = 2.0. Weak acids only partially dissociate — use Ka and the Henderson-Hasselbalch or quadratic approach. Acetic acid (Ka = 1.8×10⁻⁵): 0.1 M acetic acid has pH = ½(pKa - log 0.1) = ½(4.74 + 1) = 2.87, not 1.0 as for a strong acid.

pH Measurement Methods

pH paper: quick, semi-quantitative (±0.5 pH units). Universal indicator: visual, colorimetric. pH electrode: most accurate (±0.01 pH units). Always calibrate pH electrodes with two standard buffers (pH 4.00 and pH 7.00, or pH 7.00 and pH 10.00 depending on range) before use. Rinse electrode with distilled water between measurements. Store electrode in storage solution, never in distilled water.

Acid-Base Disorders in Medicine

The body maintains blood pH 7.35-7.45 through three systems: chemical buffers (bicarbonate, hemoglobin, phosphate), respiratory compensation (CO₂ adjustment), and renal compensation (HCO₃⁻ excretion or retention). Metabolic acidosis: low pH, low HCO₃⁻ (causes: diabetic ketoacidosis, renal failure, lactic acidosis). Respiratory acidosis: low pH, high CO₂ (causes: hypoventilation, COPD). Understanding blood gas interpretation requires mastery of pH and acid-base chemistry.

Acid-Base Chemistry and pH – Complete Reference Guide

pH is the negative base-10 logarithm of the hydrogen ion concentration: pH = -log₁₀[H⁺]. It measures acidity and basicity on a scale from 0 (most acidic) to 14 (most basic), with 7.0 representing neutral pure water at 25°C. The logarithmic scale means each unit represents a 10-fold difference in hydrogen ion concentration — pH 3 has 10 times more H⁺ than pH 4, and 100 times more than pH 5.

pH is one of the most important chemical parameters in science, medicine, and industry. It affects reaction rates, protein structure, enzyme activity, drug ionization, nutrient availability in soil, aquatic life, and virtually every biological process. Understanding pH calculation is fundamental to chemistry education and laboratory work.

Strong Acids and Bases – Direct pH Calculation

Strong acids dissociate completely: [H⁺] = initial acid concentration. HCl (0.01 M): [H⁺] = 0.01 M, pH = -log(0.01) = 2.00. H₂SO₄ (0.005 M, 2 H⁺ per molecule): [H⁺] = 0.01 M, pH = 2.00. Strong base NaOH (0.001 M): [OH⁻] = 0.001 M, pOH = 3.00, pH = 14 - 3 = 11.00. Strong acids and bases are fully ionized at any concentration — use concentration directly to find [H⁺] or [OH⁻].

Weak Acids – Henderson-Hasselbalch and Ka

Weak acids partially dissociate according to Ka. For acetic acid (CH₃COOH, Ka = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵) at 0.1 M: [H⁺] = √(Ka × C) = √(1.8 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.1) = √(1.8 × 10⁻⁶) = 1.34 × 10⁻³ M, pH = 2.87. This approximation works when Ka << C (less than 5% dissociation). For more accurate results, solve the quadratic equation: Ka = x²/(C-x). Common weak acid pKa values: acetic acid 4.74, carbonic acid 6.35, phosphoric acid 7.20, ammonium 9.25.

pH in Biological Systems

Biological systems maintain precise pH for enzyme function and protein stability. Blood: pH 7.35-7.45 (critical range — death occurs outside pH 6.8-8.0). Stomach acid: pH 1.5-3.5 (HCl secretion for protein digestion and pathogen killing). Small intestine: pH 6-7.4 (pancreatic bicarbonate neutralizes stomach acid). Urine: pH 4.5-8.0 (varies with diet and kidney function). Cytoplasm: pH 7.0-7.4. Lysosomes (cellular waste processors): pH 4.5-5.0 (acidic to activate degradative enzymes).

Measuring pH – Methods and Accuracy

pH indicator paper: quick, semi-quantitative (±0.5 pH units), inexpensive. Universal indicator solution: colorimetric, useful for teaching and rough estimates. Digital pH electrode: most accurate (±0.01 pH units), requires two-point calibration with pH 4.00 and pH 7.00 standard buffers, must be rinsed between samples, stored in 3M KCl or storage solution (never distilled water). Spectrophotometric pH indicators: precise and remotely readable, used in ocean chemistry monitoring (bromothymol blue, phenolphthalein).

Acid-Base Disorders in Clinical Medicine

Blood gas analysis interprets pH in context of CO₂ and HCO₃⁻. Normal: pH 7.40, pCO₂ 40 mmHg, HCO₃⁻ 24 mEq/L. Respiratory acidosis (high CO₂): pH <7.35, elevated pCO₂ — causes: hypoventilation, COPD, pneumonia. Metabolic acidosis (low HCO₃⁻): pH <7.35, low bicarbonate — causes: diabetic ketoacidosis, renal failure, lactic acidosis, diarrhea. Respiratory alkalosis (low CO₂): pH >7.45, low pCO₂ — cause: hyperventilation, anxiety, PE. Metabolic alkalosis (high HCO₃⁻): pH >7.45 — causes: vomiting (HCl loss), loop diuretics, excessive antacid use.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is pH? +
pH = -log[H⁺]. Below 7 = acidic, 7 = neutral, above 7 = basic. Each unit = 10× [H⁺] difference.
pH from H+ concentration? +
pH = -log[H⁺]. [H⁺]=0.001 M: pH=3. Reverse: [H⁺]=10^(-pH). At pH 4.3: [H⁺]=5.01×10⁻⁵.
pOH relation to pH? +
pH + pOH = 14 at 25°C. pH 3 → pOH=11. [OH⁻]=10^(-pOH).
What is Ka? +
Ka=[H⁺][A⁻]/[HA]. Higher Ka = stronger acid. Weak acid pH ≈ ½(pKa - log C).
Dangerous blood pH? +
Normal: 7.35-7.45. Below 7.0 = fatal cardiac arrhythmias. Above 7.8 = fatal alkalosis.
pH of pure water? +
7.0 at 25°C only. At 37°C (body temp): neutral pH ≈ 6.8.
