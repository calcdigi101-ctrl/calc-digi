Three Phase Power Calculator – kW, kVA and Current

Calculate three-phase electrical power including real power (kW), apparent power (kVA), reactive power (kVAR), line current, and power factor. Essential for industrial electrical engineering, motor sizing, and generator selection.

Three-phase electrical power is the universal standard for industrial power distribution worldwide. Every factory, data center, hospital, and large commercial building is powered by three-phase. Understanding three-phase power calculations is essential for industrial electricians, electrical engineers, facilities managers, and anyone specifying or maintaining industrial electrical equipment.

Three Phase Power Explained

Three-phase electrical power is the standard for industrial and commercial power distribution worldwide. It uses three conductors carrying alternating current 120° apart in phase. Three-phase systems are more efficient than single-phase for power transmission and motor operation — they deliver 1.732× more power using the same wire size.

Three Phase vs Single Phase

Single phase: P = V × I × PF. Three phase: P = √3 × VL × IL × PF (where √3 ≈ 1.732). The √3 factor comes from the phase relationship between the three conductors. A 400V three-phase system can deliver the same power as a 690V single-phase system with the same current.

Line Voltage vs Phase Voltage

In three-phase systems: Line voltage (VL) is measured between any two phases. Phase voltage (Vph) is measured from one phase to neutral. VL = √3 × Vph. In a 400V (line) system: Vph = 400/√3 = 231V (which is why 230V single-phase outlets come from 400V three-phase supplies).

Three Phase Systems in Industrial Applications

Three-phase power is the backbone of industrial electrical systems worldwide. Motors, compressors, welders, CNC machines, and commercial HVAC all run on three-phase. Understanding three-phase calculations is essential for anyone working in industrial electrical engineering or facilities management.

Three-Phase Motor Calculations

For a three-phase induction motor: I = P / (√3 × VL × η × PF), where η is motor efficiency. A 22 kW (30 HP) motor at 400V, 92% efficiency, 0.85 PF: I = 22,000 / (1.732 × 400 × 0.92 × 0.85) = 40.6A. Size the contactor, cables, and overload relay based on this full-load current (FLC). Starter sizing: Direct-on-line starts draw 6-8× FLC momentarily.

Unbalanced Three-Phase Systems

Ideal three-phase systems have equal loads on all three phases. In reality, single-phase loads connected to three-phase (like office lighting and computers) create imbalance. Voltage imbalance of just 3.5% causes 25% increase in motor heat — significantly reducing motor life. Keep voltage imbalance under 2% for critical motor loads.

Power Factor Correction for Three-Phase

Three-phase capacitor banks correct lagging power factor from inductive loads. A facility running at 0.75 PF consuming 500 kVA of apparent power uses only 375 kW real power. Improving PF to 0.95 reduces apparent power to 395 kVA — the same real power with 21% less current. This reduces transformer loading, cable losses, and utility bills.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the formula for three phase power? +
P (kW) = √3 × VL × IL × PF / 1000. Where VL is line-to-line voltage (V), IL is line current (A), PF is power factor (0-1). For a 400V system with 100A at PF 0.9: P = 1.732 × 400 × 100 × 0.9 / 1000 = 62.4 kW.
What is the difference between kW and kVA? +
kW (kilowatts) is real power — actual energy consumed. kVA (kilovolt-amperes) is apparent power — the total power supplied including reactive power. kW = kVA × Power Factor. A motor drawing 100 kVA at 0.85 PF consumes 85 kW of real power.
Why is three phase more efficient than single phase? +
Three-phase delivers 1.732× more power than single-phase with the same voltage and current. It also provides constant power delivery (three-phase does not pulse like single-phase), making motors smoother and more efficient. Transmission losses are lower per kW delivered.
What is power factor in three phase systems? +
Power factor (PF) = Real Power (kW) / Apparent Power (kVA) = cos(φ). Low PF (below 0.9) means the system draws more current than needed for the useful work done. Utilities often penalize industrial customers for low power factor. Capacitor banks correct low PF.
How do I calculate line current from three phase power? +
IL = P / (√3 × VL × PF). For 50 kW at 400V with PF 0.85: IL = 50,000 / (1.732 × 400 × 0.85) = 50,000 / 588.9 = 84.9 A. This is used to select fuses, cables, and contactors.
What is reactive power (kVAR)? +
Reactive power (kVAR) is power oscillating between source and inductive/capacitive loads without doing useful work. Q = √3 × VL × IL × sin(φ). Large inductive loads (motors, transformers) generate significant kVAR. Capacitor banks add negative kVAR to cancel it.
