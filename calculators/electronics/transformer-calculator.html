Transformer Design Guide

A transformer transfers electrical energy between two circuits through electromagnetic induction. It consists of two coils (primary and secondary) wound on a shared magnetic core. Transformers can step up or step down voltage while conserving power (in ideal transformers). They are fundamental to power distribution, electronics power supplies, and audio equipment.

Turns Ratio and Voltage Transformation

The turns ratio (Np:Ns) determines voltage transformation: Vs/Vp = Ns/Np. A step-down transformer with 10:1 turns ratio converts 230V to 23V. A step-up transformer with 1:10 ratio converts 120V to 1,200V. The turns ratio is the most fundamental transformer specification.

Current Transformation

Current transforms inversely to voltage: Is/Ip = Np/Ns. A step-down transformer that halves voltage doubles current. This is why power lines use high voltage (low current) for long-distance transmission — lower current means less I²R resistive losses in cables.

Transformer Design and Selection Guide

Transformers are critical components in power supplies, audio equipment, and electrical distribution. Selecting the right transformer requires understanding VA rating, regulation, core material, and frequency. A mismatch can cause overheating, excessive voltage drop under load, or audio hum.

Transformer VA Rating

Transformer capacity is rated in VA (volt-amperes), not watts. A 100VA transformer delivers 100VA regardless of load power factor. For resistive loads (VA = W), 100VA = 100W capacity. For a 12V secondary, 100VA = 12V × 8.33A maximum current. Always size the transformer at 1.2-1.5× your expected load to account for regulation and inrush current.

Transformer Regulation

Voltage regulation = (Vno-load - Vfull-load) / Vfull-load × 100%. A poorly regulated transformer may show 14V with no load but drop to 11V under full load. Regulation depends on winding resistance and leakage inductance. High-quality toroidal transformers: 3-5% regulation. Standard EI-core: 8-12%. For precision power supplies, use a voltage regulator after the transformer.

Toroidal vs EI Core Transformers

Toroidal transformers (donut-shaped core) have 50% less stray magnetic field, lower audible hum, higher efficiency (98% vs 95%), and better regulation than EI-core (stacked lamination) transformers. EI-core is cheaper to manufacture. Toroidal is preferred for audio amplifiers, medical equipment, and noise-sensitive applications. Both work at 50/60 Hz mains frequency.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does a transformer step down voltage? +
A step-down transformer has more turns on the primary than secondary (Np > Ns). Vs = Vp × (Ns/Np). For a 10:1 ratio: 230V primary → 23V secondary. Current steps up by the same ratio: Is = Ip × (Np/Ns).
What is transformer turns ratio? +
Turns ratio = Np/Ns (primary turns divided by secondary turns). A ratio of 10:1 is a step-down transformer. 1:10 is step-up. The turns ratio directly equals the voltage transformation ratio and the inverse of the current transformation ratio.
Is power conserved in a transformer? +
In an ideal transformer: Pp = Ps (primary power = secondary power). V_p × I_p = V_s × I_s. In real transformers, efficiency is 95-99% for large power transformers, with losses from copper resistance (I²R) and core magnetic hysteresis.
What is transformer efficiency? +
Real transformer efficiency = (Power out / Power in) × 100%. Large power transformers: 98-99%. Small signal transformers: 85-95%. Losses include copper losses (I²R in windings) and iron/core losses (hysteresis and eddy currents in the magnetic core).
What is impedance matching with transformers? +
Transformers transform impedance by the square of the turns ratio: Zp = Zs × (Np/Ns)². Audio output transformers match a low-impedance speaker (4-16Ω) to a high-impedance amplifier output (thousands of ohms) for maximum power transfer.
Why are power lines high voltage? +
High voltage means low current for the same power (P=VI). Lower current reduces I²R losses in transmission cables. A 1,000V line carrying 1A loses the same power as a 10V line carrying 100A, but the 10V system requires 100× thicker cables.
