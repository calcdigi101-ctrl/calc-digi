Decibel Calculator – dB, Power Ratio and Voltage

Convert decibels to power ratios, voltage ratios, and signal gain/loss. Calculate amplifier gain in dB, speaker sound pressure levels, RF signal attenuation, and audio signal chain analysis.

The decibel is an indispensable tool in electronics, acoustics, and telecommunications because human perception — of sound, light, and radio signal strength — is inherently logarithmic, not linear. Our brains perceive a tenfold increase in sound power as roughly doubling in loudness. The decibel scale naturally matches this human perceptual reality.

Formula

dB (power) = 10 × log₁₀(P2/P1) | dB (voltage) = 20 × log₁₀(V2/V1) | Power ratio = 10^(dB/10)

Decibel (dB) Guide

The decibel (dB) is a logarithmic unit expressing the ratio between two quantities — typically power or amplitude. Because human perception of sound and signal strength is logarithmic rather than linear, decibels are the natural unit for audio, RF, and telecommunications engineering. A 10 dB increase represents a 10× power increase but only sounds twice as loud to humans.

Power dB vs Voltage dB

For power: dB = 10 × log₁₀(P2/P1). For voltage (or pressure): dB = 20 × log₁₀(V2/V1). The factor of 20 for voltage (vs 10 for power) comes from the relationship P = V²/R — doubling voltage quadruples power, which is 6 dB in voltage terms but also 6 dB in power terms (consistent).

Decibels in Audio, RF, and Telecommunications

The decibel scale appears across all branches of electronics and physics. Audio engineers use dB to describe signal levels and dynamic range. RF engineers use dBm and dBi for power levels and antenna gain. Telecommunications uses dB to specify cable losses and amplifier gains. Understanding dB allows rapid mental arithmetic across many orders of magnitude.

dB in Audio Engineering

The standard reference level in professional audio is 0 dBu = 0.775V RMS. Consumer audio uses 0 dBV = 1V RMS. A mixing console shows levels in dBFS (full scale) for digital: 0 dBFS is maximum, and levels are always negative (e.g., -18 dBFS for nominal level). Dynamic range of 16-bit CD audio = 20 × log10(2^16) = 96 dB. Dynamic range of 24-bit = 144 dB.

dBm in RF Engineering

dBm = 10 × log10(P/1mW). Common reference points: smartphone transmit power 23 dBm (200mW), WiFi router 20 dBm (100mW), strong received WiFi signal -60 dBm (1nW), minimum usable WiFi -95 dBm. Link budget calculation: TX power (dBm) + TX antenna gain (dBi) - cable loss (dB) - path loss (dB) + RX antenna gain (dBi) = received signal (dBm).

Noise Floor and SNR

Thermal noise power at room temperature (Johnson-Nyquist noise): kTB where k = Boltzmann constant, T = temperature (K), B = bandwidth (Hz). At 25°C in 1 MHz bandwidth: N = -114 dBm. This sets the fundamental limit on receiver sensitivity. A receiver with 10 dB noise figure operating in 10 MHz bandwidth has noise floor = -114 + 10 + 10 = -94 dBm.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does 3 dB mean? +
3 dB represents a power ratio of approximately 2:1 (doubling). In audio, the -3 dB point of a filter is the frequency where power is halved — the standard definition of a filter cutoff frequency. A 3 dB gain means output power is twice the input.
What is the difference between dBm and dB? +
dB is a relative unit (ratio between two values). dBm is an absolute power level relative to 1 milliwatt: dBm = 10 × log₁₀(P/1mW). 0 dBm = 1mW, 10 dBm = 10mW, 30 dBm = 1W. Used in RF and telecommunications for absolute signal levels.
How loud is 60 dB in real life? +
60 dB is the level of a normal conversation at 1 meter. The scale: 0 dB (threshold of hearing), 30 dB (quiet library), 60 dB (conversation), 85 dB (city traffic), 110 dB (rock concert), 120 dB (threshold of pain), 140 dB (jet engine).
What is amplifier gain in dB? +
Gain (dB) = 20 × log₁₀(Vout/Vin) for voltage gain. A gain of 20 dB means Vout is 10× Vin. A gain of 40 dB means 100× voltage amplification. Negative dB (attenuation): -6 dB = voltage halved, -20 dB = voltage reduced to 1/10.
What is SNR (signal-to-noise ratio) in dB? +
SNR = 10 × log₁₀(Signal Power / Noise Power) dB. A 60 dB SNR means signal is 1,000,000× more powerful than noise — high quality audio. CD audio: ~96 dB SNR. 24-bit digital audio: ~144 dB SNR. FM radio: ~60 dB SNR.
How do I calculate total gain in a signal chain? +
Add dB values for each stage. A signal chain with a 20 dB amplifier, 3 dB cable loss, and 15 dB amplifier has total gain = 20 - 3 + 15 = 32 dB. This additive property makes dB incredibly convenient for multi-stage signal analysis.
