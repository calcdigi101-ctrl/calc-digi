Home Affordability Calculator

Determine how much home you can afford based on your gross income, monthly debts, down payment, and interest rate. Uses the industry-standard 28/36 debt-to-income rule used by most lenders.

Formula

Max Housing = Gross Monthly Income x 28% | Max Total Debt = Gross Monthly Income x 36%

The 28/36 rule: monthly housing costs should not exceed 28% of gross monthly income, and total monthly debts should not exceed 36%. These thresholds help ensure you can comfortably afford your home.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 28/36 rule? +
Lenders allow housing costs up to 28% of gross monthly income and total debts up to 36%. On $8,333/month income: max housing $2,333, max total debt $3,000. These determine mortgage qualification.
How much house can I afford on $100,000 salary? +
$100,000/year = $8,333/month. At 28% rule: $2,333/month for housing. At 7% for 30 years, that supports roughly a $350,000 mortgage, or more with a substantial down payment.
Does credit score affect affordability? +
Yes. A higher credit score typically qualifies you for a lower rate. Even 0.5% lower on a $300,000 loan saves over $30,000 in interest and reduces monthly payment by about $100.
What costs beyond the mortgage should I budget? +
Budget for property taxes (0.5-2.5%/yr), homeowners insurance (~$1,200/yr), HOA fees if applicable, and maintenance (1% of home value per year). These can add $500-1,500/month to housing costs.
