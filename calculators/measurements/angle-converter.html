Angle Unit Conversion Guide

Angles are measured in three main units: degrees (everyday use), radians (mathematics and physics), and gradians (surveying). The degree system (360 per circle) dates to ancient Babylon. Radians are the SI standard used in all advanced mathematics.

Degrees — The Everyday Angle

A full circle = 360 degrees. Right angle = 90 degrees. Straight angle = 180 degrees. Each degree is divided into 60 arc-minutes and 3,600 arc-seconds — used in GPS coordinates and astronomy.

Radians — The Mathematical Standard

1 radian is the angle subtended by an arc equal to the radius. A full circle = 2*pi radians. pi radians = 180 degrees. Calculus and physics formulas require radians — the derivative of sin(x) is cos(x) ONLY when x is in radians.

Gradians

A gradian (gon) divides a right angle into 100 units, making a full circle 400 gradians. Used in some European surveying systems. 1 degree = 1.1111 gradians.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you convert degrees to radians? +
Multiply degrees by pi/180 (approximately 0.017453). To convert radians to degrees, multiply by 180/pi (approximately 57.2958). Example: 90 degrees = 90 x pi/180 = pi/2 = 1.5708 radians.
What is pi radians in degrees? +
pi radians = 180 degrees exactly. 2*pi radians = 360 degrees. pi/2 radians = 90 degrees. pi/4 = 45 degrees. pi/6 = 30 degrees. pi/3 = 60 degrees. These are the key angle relationships in trigonometry.
Why do mathematicians use radians instead of degrees? +
Radians simplify calculus formulas. The derivative of sin(x) is cos(x) only when x is in radians. All Taylor series for trig functions assume radians. Physics and engineering equations are naturally simpler in radians.
What is 1 radian in degrees? +
1 radian = 180/pi = approximately 57.2958 degrees. A full circle has 2*pi = 6.2832 radians. One radian is the angle where the arc length equals the circle radius — about 57.3 degrees or roughly 1/6 of a circle.
What is a gradian or gon? +
A gradian (gon) divides a right angle into 100 units, making a full circle 400 gradians. This base-10 system simplifies some surveying calculations. 1 gradian = 0.9 degrees = pi/200 radians.
How many arc-seconds are in a degree? +
1 degree = 60 arc-minutes = 3,600 arc-seconds. Arc-seconds are used in astronomy and GPS precision. The Hubble Space Telescope resolves detail at about 0.1 arc-seconds — about 1/36,000 of a degree.
